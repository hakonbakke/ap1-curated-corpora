# Priority questions

Corpus: Area and aquaculture / Areal og havbruk

These questions were written after the 33 documents were curated, rewritten
on 2026-09-13 after the page-by-page extract walk, then tightened on
2026-09-15 against `PRIORITY_QUESTIONS_PRECISION.md`. Norwegian wording is
the working Ask text. English is the parallel. They follow what this set
contains, not a pre-set lice-style causal chain.

They are a working structure for tagging and Ask. They are **not** confirmed
by Ragnar Tveterås, Marit Schei Olsen or Tonje Osmundsen. Thord accepted the
2026-09-15 sentences as good for now.

Room object: aquaculture's claim on coastal area. Other sectors and living
nature enter when these papers name them as constraints or competing claims.
Answers must be grounded in this corpus. They need not name the farm in
every sentence.

Six papers added 2026-09-15 sit under the existing codes. They did not get
new questions. The original 33 were not retagged.

## What this room is for

The first job is to keep **disagreement about the conflict** visible. What
is scarce, whose use must give way, and whether money opens the municipal
gate are disputed in this set. Physical pen area is treated as small in
several core texts. The live fight is what then binds growth, and which
values enter the file.

That disagreement is not villaks-style camps on one estimate. Papers rarely
rebut one another on a shared number. They write past one another on the
problem definition. Honest Broker work is still to show those diagnoses,
not to pick a winner.

Gaps are the second beat. They explain why the fight does not close: knowledge
is missing, postponed, or applied unevenly. A4 is where that cluster is
strongest. Do not let the room become a research-needs memo.

A second cluster in the set is adjacent: farm-to-farm disease and lice
models, national fish-health and risk yearbooks, salmon-versus-chicken
welfare law, and the IPBES values assessment. Those files were selected in.
They are not relabelled as siting papers.

Two Kvalvik and Robertsen 2017 PDFs are the same journal article. Both
folders are kept.

## How the codes work

**A0** is the lead Ask. It is **not** a metadata tag. Do not add `A0` to
`priority_questions` until the three selectors confirm the set.

**A1-A6** remain the tagging codes. Existing tags on the original 33 files
stay as they were after page-verify. Do not retag those files on this

Samlet belastning (a new farm in already used sea) is named under A3 and

IPBES stays on values in trade-offs (A3). It is not a seventh theme.

## A0. What is actually scarce (lead Ask, not a tag)

**Question (Norwegian)**
Hva begrenser vekst i havbruk på norskekysten: mangel på ledig sjøflate, produksjons- og lokalitetstillatelser, kommunal utpeking av brukbar sjø, eller biologi (lus, sykdom, tetthet, velferd)?

**Question (English)**
What limits aquaculture growth on the Norwegian coast: lack of unused sea surface, production and locality licences, municipal designation of usable sea, or biology (lice, disease, density, welfare)?

**Why this question matters**
Testers and decision-makers will ask whether there is enough area. If Ask
answers as if the scarce object were already agreed, the room hides the
main problem. The set agrees that pen surface is a tiny share of inshore
sea. It does not agree on what then binds growth.

**What this set already shows**
Hersoug, Mikkelsen and Osmundsen (2020) say the national limit at the time
was mainly production-licence capacity and traffic-light growth, not that
unused sea surface had run out, though sanitary spacing and other uses shrink
usable water, and some
regions are full. Sandersen and Kvalvik (2015) and Gullestad et al. (2011)
treat good sites and municipal coastal-zone designation as the scarce
object. Sand and Misund (2025) and Kulmambetova and Tveterås (2025) put
lice, disease, welfare and proximity first. The 2023 bærekraftig report
wants density rules and disputes traffic-light cuts. Osmundsen and Olsen
(2025) add that counties lack tools to change the existing site structure.
Jentoft and Buanes (2005) name the myth that coastal space is almost
unlimited: from the air the pens look like dots, on the ground security
zones make aquaculture space-consuming.

**Where views differ**
The difference is a disputed problem definition, not a rebuttal chain.
Hersoug, Mikkelsen and Osmundsen put licences and traffic-light first.
Sandersen and Kvalvik, and Gullestad, put good sites and municipal
designation first. Sand and Kulmambetova put lice, disease, welfare and
density first. Authors write past one another more than they argue on one
estimate. Keep the three diagnoses visible. Do not pick a winner.

**Gaps**
No document in the set tests the three diagnoses against one another on
the same coast and the same years. Whether an area rent or production fee
unlocks designation is left open (Sandersen 2015, Hersoug 2020). How much
closed, land-based or offshore farming would cut coastal demand is
qualitative. Carrying capacity by production area is little known (HI
2026). The set does not settle which constraint binds in which region.

**Do not tag**
Answer A0 by retrieving across A1, A2, A5 and A6. A4 matters when the
file postpones the knowledge that would distinguish those diagnoses.

## A1. Planning and designation

**Question (Norwegian)**
Når kommunen peker ut, eller nekter å peke ut, sjøareal til havbruk, hva avgjør dette: selve plankartet, senere dispensasjon, eller innsigelse og avslag fra sektormyndighet etter at kartet er vedtatt?

**Question (English)**
When a municipality designates, or refuses to designate, sea area for aquaculture, what decides this: the plan map itself, later dispensation, or sector objection and refusal after the map is adopted?

**Why this question matters**
Since the Planning and Building Act revision of 1989, municipalities
designate aquaculture sea. That is the gate that national growth targets
must pass. Plans can open water, freeze it as FFFNA search space, or fail
to be made at all.

**What this set already shows**
Municipal plans under the Planning and Building Act apply to one nautical
mile beyond the baselines (Hersoug 2020). Inter-municipal planning is
described as the most promising inside-the-box change, and as uneven in
practice (Hersoug 2020, Kvalvik and Robertsen 2017, Sand 2025 on Namdalen
and Fosen). Combined-purpose zoning can look large on the map and still
fail at application (Sand 2025 on Osen). County objections and sector
vetoes sit on top of the municipal map (rettslig rammeverk 2015, Osmundsen
and Olsen 2025). Sørdahl and Kvalvik (2024) and Sørdahl (2026) read five
generations of Tromsø plans: more comprehensive documents do not by
themselves settle what sea is designated.

**Where views differ**
Writers agree that the municipality designates. They differ on whether
weak plans, weak follow-up, or later sector refusal is the binding step.
That is a fight about where the conflict sits in the process, not a camp
split on one estimate.

**Gaps**
After a plan is adopted, day-to-day management and dispensations can
reopen undesignated sea (Kvalvik and Robertsen 2017, SALT 1065). The set
does not follow a sample of FFFNA or combined-purpose areas through to
granted or refused localities. It does not measure whether inter-municipal
plans actually allocate water or only coordinate process. Plan-side impact
assessment versus Aquaculture Act licensing remains split (SALT 1065).

**Evidence types needed**
`policy_assessment`, `legal_analysis`, `empirical_observational`

**Key documents in this set**
Kvalvik and Robertsen 2017, Sandersen Helgeland 2021, Mikkelsen 2019 and
2022, SALT 1065, SALT 1075, Osmundsen and Olsen 2025, rettslig rammeverk
2015, Gullestad 2011, bærekraftig 2023, Sand 2025, Sørdahl and Kvalvik 2024,
Jentoft and Buanes 2005.

## A2. Licences and locality clearance

**Question (Norwegian)**
Hvordan former produksjonstillatelse, lokalitetstillatelse og fylkesbehandling etter akvakulturloven hvem som får en lokalitet, og hvor i den stabelen saken dør?

**Question (English)**
How do production licences, locality licences and county administration of the Aquaculture Act shape who gets a site, and where in that stack does the case die?

**Why this question matters**
A farm needs both a production licence and a locality licence. Counties
coordinate the locality permit. Several sector authorities can refuse.
National growth can stall on the locality file even when company biomass
exists.

**What this set already shows**
Hersoug (2022) maps ten licensing systems. Osmundsen and Olsen (2025)
separate county coordination from municipal designation and from sector
vetoes. SALT 1110 reads 251 refusal cases from 2020-2025. Mattilsynet is
the most frequent recorded refuser. Lice infection risk is the largest
coded Mattilsynet ground. In many files facts, professional judgement and
precaution run together, so the decisive ground is hard to name. Coral
density thresholds in use are not standardised. Political county bodies
can refuse after all sectors have granted, or grant after the
administration has refused. Sandersen and Kvalvik (2014) find that the 2010
transfer of aquaculture tasks to County Councils was limited devolution:
more units, little real authority. Schütz and Slater (2019) treat Norwegian
production areas and the plan-to-licence link as a predictability problem.

**Where views differ**
The live tension is whether locality scarcity is a licence-growth problem,
a veterinary-distance problem, or an opaque-refusal problem. The texts
describe a stacked system more than they rebut one another. SALT 1110
makes that opacity a finding, not a side note.

**Gaps**
FAKS is incomplete. The legal sample in SALT 1110 under-represents
Trøndelag. There is no public decision database that would let a later
study rematch plan designation to outcome. Capacity-utilisation data and a
shared kurant definition are missing (Osmundsen and Olsen 2025). The set
does not show how often a site dies at plan, at sector veto, or at county
discretion.

**Evidence types needed**
`legal_analysis`, `policy_assessment`, `empirical_observational`

**Key documents in this set**
Hersoug 2022, Hersoug 2020, Osmundsen and Olsen 2025, SALT 1110, rettslig
rammeverk 2015, Gullestad 2011, Osmundsen 2021 (shared waters), bærekraftig
2023, Sandersen and Kvalvik 2014, Schütz and Slater 2019.

## A3. Trade-offs and coexistence

**Question (Norwegian)**
Når sjø går til havbruksanlegg, hva må vike (annen bruk, levende natur, eller anlegget), og hvordan tas samlet belastning inn når merden kommer i sjø som allerede brukes?

**Question (English)**
When sea goes to an aquaculture farm, what must yield (other use, living nature, or the farm), and how is cumulative load taken in when the pen enters sea that is already in use?

**Why this question matters**
Arealkonflikt is not only a map of empty cells. It is a claim about what
must give way: fisheries, shipping, recreation, wild salmon, coastal cod,
seabed nature, or the farm itself. Cumulative load is the legal and
planning form of that claim.

**What this set already shows**
Evenset et al. (2023) review coexistence stressors and state that
published literature on direct harm from other coastal industries to farms
was not found. Expert scores fill that hole. Mikkelsen et al. (2025) is
the northern-law account of samlet belastning and what the Aquaculture Act
does and does not require. Sand 2025 lists conflict types for Trøndelag
(wild salmon and coastal cod, kelp, land-based works, offshore, defence,
marine protection). Rosendal (2025) and Sandersen and Kvalvik (2015) put
environment and local economy in the same municipal choice. IPBES (2022)
says valuation studies rarely report uptake (under 5 percent). Box 4.3
(Winge) ranks nature below local development and energy in a Norwegian
example. Schütz and Slater (2019) place aquaculture beside offshore wind in
the same legal allocation problem. Meld. St. 35 (2023-2024) places
aquaculture as one sector inside national use and conservation of nature.

**Where views differ**
This is the arealkonflikt tag. Fisheries, shipping, recreation, wild fish,
seabed nature, local economy and the farm are competing claims about what
must give way. Sandersen and Kvalvik treat the environmental debate and
the rent debate as parallel, not as one. IPBES and municipal practice
diverge on whether nature values enter the file. Those are value and use
conflicts, not one scientific camp split. IPBES is peripheral (two files).
Do not let it become the room.

**Gaps**
Activity to stressor to environmental effect, including combined effects,
is named as missing science (Evenset 2023). GIS cumulative mapping is not
feasible with current open data. Mikkelsen et al. (2025) report that a
large share of assessed cases were weak on stressor interaction. There is
still no published literature in this set on other industries harming
farms. IPBES uptake into Norwegian siting files is not studied here.
Seabird refusals in SALT 1110 often rest on general disturbance rather
than site-specific population effect.

**Evidence types needed**
`review`, `policy_assessment`, `legal_analysis`, `empirical_observational`

**Key documents in this set**
Evenset 2023, Mikkelsen 2025, Sand 2025 pair, Kvalvik 2017, Rosendal 2025,
Sandersen 2015, Karlsen 2019, Osmundsen 2021, IPBES 2022, SALT 1075,
SALT 1110, Schütz and Slater 2019, Meld. St. 35 (2023-2024).

## A4. Knowledge in decisions

**Question (Norwegian)**
Hvilken kunnskap brukes, mangler, utsettes eller brukes skjevt i plan, KU og lokalitetsbehandling av havbruk, og hvordan skal usikkerhet og føre var inn i vedtaket?

**Question (English)**
What knowledge is used, missing, postponed or applied unevenly in plans, impact assessment and locality processing of aquaculture, and how should uncertainty and precaution enter the decision?

**Why this question matters**
If the scarce object is disputed, the file is where that dispute should
be resolved. In this set the file often postpones the hard part, or uses
precaution and general statements instead of site-specific effect.

**What this set already shows**
SALT 1065 is the planning-side account of what belongs in municipal KU
versus Aquaculture Act licensing, including Ombudsman cases on uncertainty
in the plan file. SALT 1110 is the refusal-side companion: coral
thresholds, lice history and precaution are not standardised. Evenset
2023 and Mikkelsen 2025 state what combined-effects knowledge would
require. Metier 2023 treats marine grunnkart as knowledge infrastructure
for plans and locality choice. Sandersen Helgeland 2021 and Mikkelsen
2022 show how knowledge and process look in regional planning. Sørdahl
(2024, 2026) argue that thicker, more holistic plans are not themselves a
decision.

**Where views differ**
The live disagreement is how uncertainty and precaution should enter the
file, not what the missing study would find. SALT 1110 says precaution is
applied differently across cases and is not meant to be used for safety's
sake. SALT 1065 records Ombudsman conflict over uncertainty in the plan
file. Keep used, missing, postponed and unevenly applied distinct. Do not
flatten them into «we need more research».

**Gaps**
This is the strongest gap cluster in the room. Plan KU versus licence
assessment is not closed. Coral density and lice-history use vary by
case. High-resolution coastal climate series are missing (SALT 1075).
Cage-type data and mature closed technology evidence are thin
(bærekraftig 2023). Institute risk reports are used as diagnoses in
decisions, not as operational gap-closing plans (SALT 1110).

**Evidence types needed**
`policy_assessment`, `legal_analysis`, `review`, `methods`

**Key documents in this set**
SALT 1065, SALT 1110, Evenset 2023, Metier 2023, Mikkelsen 2022, Mikkelsen
2025, Sandersen Helgeland 2021, SALT 1075, Osmundsen and Olsen 2025,
Sørdahl and Kvalvik 2024, Sørdahl 2026.

## A5. Density and biosecurity

**Question (Norwegian)**
Hva følger av hvor og hvor tett havbruksanleggene ligger for produktivitet, sykdom, lus og belastning, og er «mer areal» feil grep hvis avstand og biologi binder først?

**Question (English)**
What follows from how densely and where aquaculture farms sit for productivity, disease, lice and load, and is more area the wrong move if distance and biology bind first?

**Why this question matters**
Sanitary distance and neighbour infection shrink usable water far more
than the pen itself. If biology binds first, «more area» is the wrong
policy sentence. If distance rules are the real occupation, the map of
empty fjord is misleading.

**What this set already shows**
Hersoug 2020 and Sand 2025 repeat the small pen-surface figure and the
much larger spacing occupation. Kulmambetova and Tveterås (2025) test
spatial density and productivity. Gullestad 2011 proposed production
areas and coordinated fallowing without a settled zone size. The 2023
bærekraftig report treats inshore growth, density and biosecurity as one
problem. HI 2026 scores infection coupling and influence area by
production area. Adjacent papers (Aldrin 2011, 2015, 2017, Qviller 2024,
Moldal 2026) model farm-to-farm spread or compile national health.

**Where views differ**
If biology and neighbour distance bind first, «more area» is the wrong
sentence. If empty fjord is the picture, spacing rules look like
overreach. The 2023 bærekraftig report wants density rules and disputes
traffic-light cuts. HI 2026 treats carrying capacity by production area
as little known. Keep density-as-area papers separate from adjacent
farm-to-farm epidemiology. Do not turn A5 into a second villaks room.

**Gaps**
How large a coordinated-fallowing zone must be was left open in 2011.
Lice and escape indicators were not operational then. Loss-rule triggers
were left to politics (Gullestad). Traffic-light is lice on wild
salmonids, while bath treatments can stay toxic over kilometres (Evenset
2023). Hard-bottom organics, copper and escaped cleaner fish are named as
under-used in rules. Adjacent epidemiology papers do not answer siting.

**Evidence types needed**
`empirical_observational`, `model`, `risk_assessment`, `policy_assessment`

**Key documents in this set**
Kulmambetova 2025, Gullestad 2011, Hersoug 2020, Sand 2025, bærekraftig
2023, SALT 1075, HI 2026. Adjacent: Aldrin 2011/2015/2017, Qviller 2024,
Moldal 2026.

## A6. Incentives and municipal politics

**Question (Norwegian)**
Hvordan påvirker skatt, fond og arealavgiftsdebatten kommunenes vilje til å huse havbruksanlegg, og kan kommunen ta pengene uten å åpne mer sjøareal?

**Question (English)**
How do taxes, funds and the area-rent debate affect municipalities' willingness to host aquaculture farms, and can the municipality take the money without opening more sea area?

**Why this question matters**
If municipalities designate sea, national targets depend on local
willingness. Money is the proposed fix. The set does not show that the
fix works.

**What this set already shows**
Sandersen and Kvalvik (2015) treat area rent as a wicked problem:
environment, other uses, and modest local return after industry
concentration. An inter-ministerial process concluded that area rent was
not a suited instrument. Property tax on sea installations gave modest
return. A worst-case they name is that municipalities take the rent and
then decide they already have enough area. Hersoug 2020 follows the later
production fee and the cut in the municipal share of new-capacity
payments. Rosendal (2025) links municipal environment and economy.
Rettslig rammeverk (2015) treats municipal payment as a standing
constraint. Osmundsen and Olsen (2025) note Havbruksfondet as a knowledge
and process resource.

**Where views differ**
Industry, municipalities and ministries disagree on tax design in the
source texts. Sandersen and Kvalvik report that some municipalities would
refuse new area even with pay, and that a rent could be taken without
opening the gate. Others treat a fee or Havbruksfondet as the lever that
makes designation politically possible. That is political conflict
reported by the papers, not a scientific camp split.

**Gaps**
Whether an area rent, production fee or Havbruksfondet actually increases
designation is untested in this set. The debate itself may delay
designations. Gullestad left fee design closed. SALT 1065 only recommends
earmarking funds for planning competence. There is no before-and-after
study of municipal behaviour under the fee that exists.

**Evidence types needed**
`policy_assessment`, `empirical_observational`, `legal_analysis`

**Key documents in this set**
Sandersen and Kvalvik 2015, Hersoug 2020, Rosendal 2025, rettslig
rammeverk 2015, Osmundsen and Olsen 2025, bærekraftig 2023, Mikkelsen
2019.

## What we did not add

A seventh tag for IPBES values would overweight two peripheral files.

A seventh tag for samlet belastning would split a live gap that already
sits in A3 and A4, where Evenset 2023, Mikkelsen 2025 and SALT 1110 meet.

Matching villaks at ten questions would fake a causal chain this set does
not have.

The six papers added 2026-09-15 did not get new codes. Jentoft sits under
A0 and A1, Sandersen 2014 under A2, Schütz under A2 and A3, Sørdahl under
A1 and A4, Meld. St. 35 under A3.

Technology and climate remain qualitative notes under A0, A4 and A5. They
are not their own question until the selectors add papers that test them.

## Tagging rule until selectors confirm

Each included document stays tagged with the A1-A6 questions it actually
speaks to. Peripheral files may have one tag only. Broad reports
(Gullestad, SALT 1065) may carry many tags. That is a smell if the codes
are meant to discriminate. It is acceptable while A0 is the lead Ask and
A1-A6 are a map.

If the three selectors confirm A0 as a tag, retag then, not before.
