### Background
Fiskehelserapporten 2025 is Veterinærinstituttet's official yearbook of fish health in Norway (rapportserie 5a/2026). Redaksjon ended 11 March 2026. The report was published the same day on vetinst.no. The named persons on the title pages are the editorial group. Chapter authors are credited in each chapter. Named exceptions: NIVA wrote the water-quality section, NOFIMA contributed to the climate chapter, and Pharmaq Analytic contributed to the mycobacteriosis section. The object is health, mortality, listed and non-listed disease, lice, welfare, and related production problems in farmed fish. Coastal-zone planning and municipal site designation are not the main object.

### Methods
The report compiles monthly official reports to Mattilsynet and Fiskeridirektoratet, laboratory detections at the institute and at PatoGen, Pharmaq Analytiq and Blue Analytics (about 24 companies, 692 sites in the compiled non-listed set), the aquaculture register, weekly lice and treatment reports, a survey of fish-health personnel and Mattilsynet staff (361 invited, 143 completed, 40 percent), and voluntary AquaCloud cause-of-death data. It is written mainly without in-text references. Readers are told to contact chapter authors for sources. Chapter 5 lists references. From 2017 most maps and tables use the 13 production areas in the production-area regulation. Official listed-disease counts are new detections on a site after fallowing. This curation walked the front matter, chapters 1-14, and appendices A1-E2. Charts that exist only as images are unreadable here.

### Main findings
Land-based deaths of fish over 3 grams fell for salmon (43.3 million) and rainbow trout (2.84 million) and rose for cod (2.44 million), especially in the 36-155 gram class. In the sea, 54.8 million salmon, 3.2 million rainbow trout and 2.4 million cod were registered as dead. Total salmon sea-phase losses including other categories were 67.5 million. Calculated annual sea-phase mortality risk was 14.2 percent for salmon (1.2 percentage points below 2024, lowest in at least five years), 17.0 percent for rainbow trout (up 2.0 points) and 20.4 percent for cod (down 1.8 points, still above 2021-2023). Completed salmon cycles that ended in 2025 had 14.7 percent risk. The text warns that a lower 12-month sea rate does not by itself prove lower first-feeding-to-slaughter mortality, because larger smolt and a longer land phase change time at sea.

Geographic differences are large. Salmon mortality risk was 20.1 percent in production area 4 and 9.4 percent in areas 12-13 combined. Area 6 fell by almost 8 points from an unusual 22.3 percent in 2024. Area 9 rose by about 7 points to 16.1 percent, with six confirmed ISA outbreaks, one ISA suspicion, a high share of losses coded as other (40 percent), and an algal bloom, despite relatively low biomass density. Areas 1-2 rose from 13.6 to 18.2 percent, linked in the text to non-medicinal delousing. Area 3, described as the area with the highest biomass density and among the two areas most affected by the traffic-light system, fell from 25.7 percent in 2023 to 15.2 percent in 2025. Whether low northern mortality reflects climate or lower production density is left open. 833 salmon sea sites were active in at least one month. Mean monthly salmon biomass was about 876,000 tonnes, the highest in the five-year series.

AquaCloud data cover 408 sites (about 49 percent of salmon sea sites): injuries 33.5 percent, infectious disease 28.1 percent, unknown 27.4 percent. Injuries rose relative to 2024. Among infections, HSMB and CMS rose. HSMB was detected at 167 sites and CMS at 114. IPN was detected at 40 sites, about three times 2024, including variants described as better adapted to QTL-resistant eggs. Winter-sore detections fell (Moritella viscosa 246 sites, Tenacibaculum 147). BKD fell to one confirmed outbreak. Furunculosis was not detected in farming. Piscirickettsiosis was listed in category G in 2025. Survey ranks for sea salmon still put gill disease and delousing injuries first. Nephrocalcinosis and haemorrhagic smolt syndrome dominate hatchery ranks.

ISA was confirmed at 18 sea sites, with three additional sea suspicions and an unresolved land-based broodstock suspicion. A large share was in Vesterålen, with two virus variants. The text states that suspected infected fish were moved between sites, and that infection is otherwise assumed to have occurred between neighbouring sites. Several outbreaks may relate to ILAV HPR0 in the hatchery (HPR0 at 78 sites). Seven of 18 confirmed outbreaks were on vaccinated fish. About 190 million ISA vaccine doses were dispensed. PD fell to 44 new cases, all inside the endemic zone (SAV3 in areas 3-4, SAV2 in areas 5-6). The area 8 restriction zone was lifted in November 2025. No new PD in area 2 since 2022.

Salmon-lice larval output in the spring low-limit period rose from 2024 to 2025 in ten of 13 production areas. Non-medicinal delousing weeks reached 3918 (up 24 percent from 3163), the highest in the series. Adult female lice totals by area are said to be driven largely by farmed-fish numbers. AGD was detected as far north as Troms, with more detections than in 2024. Parvicapsulosis remains a Troms and Finnmark problem and has also been found further south. A Chrysochromulina leadbeateri and Phaeocystis bloom in Nord-Norge in spring 2025 caused about two million dead farmed fish, according to Fiskeridirektoratet (7.5 million in the 2019 bloom). An autumn event around Flekkefjord is described as a probable algal bloom.

Open cages create two-way farmed-wild infection risk. High numbers and density of susceptible hosts allow amplification and spill-back. For BKD, wild salmonids are treated as the likely source of introductions, while farming raises pressure back on wild fish. Culture from 2025 PCR-positive fish in Orkla did not succeed. Chapter 5 (first climate chapter) says knowledge is not good enough to state how climate change will affect commercial fish health. Adaptation bullets include area management: planning of new localities should include expected temperature, local ecosystem dynamics, infection or lice pressure, and species tolerance. Survey respondents most often reported more need for lice treatment (81 percent of 126). Chapter 12 states that interspecies transfer (IPNV from cod to other species in a cohabitation trial) should be considered when planning multi-species sites and coastal-zone planning. That is one sentence.

About 18.9 million cleaner fish were released and 12.7 million registered dead. No cleaner fish were released in areas 9-13. Data quality does not support a claim that the death share fell. Superior share for salmon was 86.9 percent (better than 2024). Cod superior share fell to 73 percent.

Sommerset's preface describes Meld. St. 24 (2024-2025) as a proposed shift toward management based on measurable environmental impact, including lice-larva emission quotas and a fee on lost fish. A Storting majority backed the direction but put the most extensive measures on hold. The animal-welfare white paper is cited for a five-percent mortality ambition. Chapter 6 states that the traffic-light system as now designed challenges farmed-fish welfare. Those are descriptions and proposals, not evaluations in this yearbook.

### Uncertainty and limitations
Hatchery and sea registers cannot be joined at fish-group level. Official disease totals miss fish still infected from the prior year. AquaCloud coverage is partial. Non-listed counts can under-report field diagnoses. Cleaner-fish losses mix death and removal. Most figures in the extract are images. The yearbook is not a new model of area use, licensing, or municipal coastal plans.

### Relevance to this corpus
Adjacent. Production-area geography, neighbour-site ISA, endemic PD zones, lice larval output by area, farmed-wild density wording, and a few sentences on locality and coastal-zone planning are the area hooks (A5, and A3 for farmed-wild and wild-smolt lice pressure). National fish health is the object. Do not treat this as a siting paper.

### Relation to other documents
Read with Havforskningsinstituttet's 2026 risk report for the same production year. The Aldrin ISA and PD papers model neighbourhood spread. This report records 2025 outbreak counts and qualitative neighbour-site wording. It does not re-estimate those models. Gismervik et al. 2020 compares salmon and chicken welfare law. Gismervik is first author of this year's welfare chapter, which treats traffic-light growth as a welfare pressure on farmed fish.
