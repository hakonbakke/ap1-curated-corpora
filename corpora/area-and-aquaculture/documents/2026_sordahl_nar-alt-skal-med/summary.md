### Background
Sørdahl's PhD (UiT, Faculty of Humanities, Social Sciences and Education, May 2026) studies how the concept of integrated (helhetlig) planning is created and how it becomes concrete planning tools. The empirical field is Norwegian coastal-zone planning of nearshore sea. The title is Når alt skal med. Planning is treated as an activity at the intersection of politics and administration, not as a neutral technical instrument.

### Methods
The dissertation comprises three articles plus a kappa. Paper I uses institutional theory on the introduction of the ecosystem-services framework in Norwegian governance. Paper II uses governmentality on five generations of Tromsø coastal plans (this is the 2024 Maritime Studies article already in this room). Paper III conceptualises integration and its counterpart fragmentation. The kappa discusses how ambitions of more holistic planning are operationalised, how law and steering structures enable or block them, and a holism-versus-reductionism paradox.

### Main findings
Coastal areas are under increasing pressure. Area is a limited good. Fisheries, aquaculture, tourism, energy and nature conservation compete for much the same water, which produces tension and conflict.

There is long agreement that more holistic management is the goal, and little agreement on what holism means or how far it has come. Popular terms such as ecosystem-based management, sustainable development and holism can be catchwords with political recognition and little content for administration.

The dissertation finds tension between two approaches to holism. A scientific approach seeks to simplify and standardise. A political approach keeps expanding what planning must include. That produces gaps between ambitions and realities, and between ideals and institutional frames. Holism is normative, not a technical solution. What is included and excluded in practice, and which tools municipalities actually have, must be named.

Attempts to handle coastal complexity by bringing more themes under one umbrella breed more complexity: more voices, more considerations to weigh, and someone found too light. Decisions and establishments still happen, so conflicts can be said to be handled even when the conflict picture remains.

Planning as a technical instrument cannot deliver holism by itself. Final competence in municipal planning sits with the municipal council. The result in practice is a limited or reductionist holism, depending on the goal and the resources. The author also asks whether a lasting gap between the holism goal and the need to prioritise can weaken the institutional sustainability of planning (clear roles, predictability, participation, decisions that give direction).

Holism is also defended as a shared, flexible frame that can persist when political winds change, provided ambitions stay sober. The same breadth that makes the concept durable makes it dangerous because it looks unifying, positive and legitimate.

### Uncertainty and limitations
Kappa plus three articles. This curation walked the Norwegian and English summaries, the table of contents, the introduction and chapter 7. Appended article PDFs inside the thesis were not re-walked page by page here. Paper II is curated in full as 2024_sordahl_when-all-you-have-is-a-hammer. Transferability is discussed as a limit in section 7.3.

### Relevance to this corpus
This is the fullest statement in the set that coastal allocation is a political weighing, that holism can hide that weighing, and that municipalities do not have tools for everything the ambition names.
