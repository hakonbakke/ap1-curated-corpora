### Background
Sandersen and Kvalvik discuss the 2010 Norwegian administrative reform (forvaltningsreformen) and its effects on aquaculture and coastal-area governance. The reform moved selected planning, coordinating and decision-making tasks from the Directorate of Fisheries' regional offices to the County Councils, aiming at more self-governance through decentralisation. International coastal and marine management was moving the other way, toward integrated and ecosystem-based approaches on larger geographic units. The paper asks whether the reform fosters or impedes sustainable aquaculture governance and more holistic coastal management.

### Methods
This is an initial study based on multi-level governance, integrated coastal-zone management (ICZM) and ecosystem-based management (EBM) literatures, plus Norwegian policy documents and examples (including Nordland objections to a Narvik plan, and green-licence allocation that largely bypassed counties). It is not a new statistical evaluation of processing times. Coastal Management 42:447-463. Published online 5 September 2014.

### Main findings
Aquaculture produced more than 1 million tons per year with export value of 4 billion euros (Norwegian Seafood Council 2013 cited). Challenges named are nutrient wastes, disease, escapes and lice, plus need for more and larger sites (Expert Committee 2011, which is Gullestad). The siting regime is described as highly complex, with many acts and agencies (Food Safety Authority, Coastal Administration, County Governor, and NVE for hatchery water).

From 1 January 2010, counties took on aquaculture tasks. The paper counts 17 sea-bordering counties and 276 sea-bordering municipalities, of which 14 and 160 had salmon and trout licences. Decision-making units went from seven Directorate regional offices to fourteen coastal County Councils, narrowing geographic scope. Municipalities had already planned coastal zones for about 15 years, often restricted by sector objections.

The authors find mixed effects. De facto transfer of decision-making authority is described as limited, mainly administrative tasks. Specialised sectors remain largely unchanged. Horizontal coordination between counties is more likely to be reduced because units are smaller and more numerous, producing a danger of more fragmented aquaculture management. For EBM, the reform may hamper larger, connected, ecologically sound areas. For local ICZM, a small increase in horizontal coordination among municipalities is called a step forward. Real devolution of authority to counties is said not to have occurred. Counties as broad democratic institutions may still improve holistic municipal-plan coordination.

The conclusion is that the reform is more likely to improve coordination and ICZM at municipal level, and less likely to facilitate larger, cross-county EBM.

### Uncertainty and limitations
Initial, theory-led study of possible consequences, not a later process evaluation (Osmundsen and Olsen 2025 fills that later gap). Figures 1 and 2 are images. Green-licence process is used as an example of limited county role, with a coalition-politics note.

### Relevance to this corpus
This is the 2010-reform companion to Osmundsen and Olsen (2025). It states the dilemma between territorial/political coordination (ICZM, municipalities) and functional/sectoral coordination (EBM, production zones).

### Relation to other documents
Cites Jentoft and Buanes 2005 and Gullestad 2011. Read with Osmundsen and Olsen 2025 on how counties actually administered the Aquaculture Act after 2010, and Hersoug 2022 on licences above locality rights.
