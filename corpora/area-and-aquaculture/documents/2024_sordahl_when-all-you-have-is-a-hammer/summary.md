### Background
Sørdahl and Kvalvik examine how integration as a concept in coastal-zone planning is framed by government and how that framing changes over time. The empirical case is five generations of municipal coastal-zone plans in Tromsø spanning 35 years. They use a governmentality frame: problematisations, rationalisations and technologies of governing. The title point is that when integration is treated as the undisputed answer to coastal conflict, failure to settle those conflicts becomes an argument for yet more integration, not a test of whether integration is the right ambition.

### Methods
In-depth document study of Tromsø municipal and inter-municipal coastal plans: 1991 municipal plan spatial section, 2000 sea-area plan, 2008 district spatial plan, 2015 Kystplan Tromsøregionen (Balsfjord, Karlsøy, Lyngen, Målselv, Tromsø), 2022 Kystplan Tromsøregionen (Balsfjord, Karlsøy, Tromsø). National expectations documents from 2011, 2015 and 2019-2023 are used as government framing. Maritime Studies (2024) 23:39. Received 12 October 2023, accepted 18 July 2024, published 14 August 2024. Funding: Research Council of Norway grant 294799 and CLEAN (FRAM). Competing interests: none declared. SIKT ethics approval. Open access.

### Main findings
Integration is not a given. Previous spatial dispositions themselves become problems at later dates. Ambitions for integrated or comprehensive planning can obscure the purpose of planning and increase complexity.

Tromsø plans shifted vocabulary from coordination (2000 and 2008) to comprehensiveness and integration (2015) and almost solely comprehensive in 2022. Documents became thicker: the 2015 plan description was twice the 2000 plan, the 2022 plan triple. Time to develop plans increased and then became part of the rationalisation for better planning. Later generations are more standardised and follow national guidelines more closely.

Tourism and recreation moved conflict lines closer to land. The Water Regulation (EU Water Framework Directive in Norwegian law, marine water to one nautical mile from the baseline) was incorporated as a tool for integrated use and protection, and also required coordination with a new institutional setup.

The authors name a paradox of integration: bringing more issues under one umbrella does not necessarily reduce complexity. Comprehensiveness is described as an intuitively positive but hollow term. Coordination and cooperation are called more precise. Integration can be co-opted by vested interests. Failure to hinder spatial conflicts becomes a foundation for yet more comprehensiveness rather than a discussion of whether the planning system can handle those conflicts.

### Uncertainty and limitations
Single-municipality (plus neighbours) case. Governmentality reading of documents, not a causal test of whether thicker plans reduced conflict. One competing-interest statement uses 'the author' in the singular while the byline has two authors.

### Relevance to this corpus
This is the named critique that more holistic coastal planning can hide priorities and grow complexity. It is Article II of Sørdahl's 2026 dissertation in this room.
