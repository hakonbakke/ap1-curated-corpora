### Background
Sandersen analyses Kystplan Helgeland (KPH), an inter-municipal coastal-zone project from 2012 to 2019. Thirteen municipalities started. Brønnøy and Vefsn left. The plan area is about 10 000 km2, about 12 500 islands and islets, and a coastline of about 9 800 km (about 5 percent of Norway's coastline).

### Methods
Document studies and 28 qualitative interviews with KPH leadership (5), municipalities (12), Nordland Fylkes Fiskarlag (1), Nordland county (5), regional state in Nordland (4) and one ministry contact, plus meeting observation. Farm-company experiences are not discussed.

### Main findings
Coastal-zone planning is the municipalities' main tool for governing the coast. The steering group did not receive delegated process authority. KPH used a reversed method: first identify sea where aquaculture is not wanted, then designate most remaining sea as multi-use including aquaculture. The first hearing draft assigned 83 percent of sea area this way, with a general rule that special reasons were required to refuse a farm. Areas shallower than 20 m (13 percent) banned food-fish farming but opened for new technology and species. Those shallow-water openings were later dropped.

Sector authorities and the County Governor argued this gave too little conflict clearance and made impact assessment of the whole zone hard. Final conflict clearance would move to locality processing under the Aquaculture Act. First hearing: 56 objections, 39 planning-defect notes, 70 planning advices. All municipalities got objections to the special-reasons rule and to weak assessments of national and regional values (seabirds, traffic, recreation).

After a stalled year, a new project leader in 2018 mediated. Some multi-use zones lost the aquaculture letter, some dedicated A-zones were added, and new impact assessments were done. The special-reasons rule was supplemented: aquaculture is not allowed if it has substantial negative consequence for registered national or regional interests, or if consequences are uncertain. The author treats that add-on as probably weak because it is unclear who judges. Plans were adopted in 2019 with a common map. Large multi-use zones remained. The phrase omvendt metodikk was dropped.

Sandersen concludes the result is standardised, aquaculture-oriented plans, not holistic inter-municipal planning. An informant said the plan is consistent and homogeneous, not holistic. Multi-use zones were described as search areas. Sequence rules, farm-number or distance caps, species limits and cumulative load were not used. Fisheries generally received large reserved areas. Passive-gear grounds were mainly opened for aquaculture. Several informants said environment, recreation and tourism got little space. Inner-fjord municipalities were less farm-friendly than outer-coast municipalities. Brønnøy left because sea was not planned with land. Vefsn left wanting a less farm-friendly approach. Voluntary surrender of detailed siting is described as a possible long-term risk to municipal self-determination. The author does not reject the method in principle, but finds it a poor core method for a large standardised project.

### Uncertainty and limitations
How many new large sites the open zones will actually yield, given existing locality structure, is left bounded. Nature-value knowledge in large zones is described as thin. Farm-side outcomes are not evaluated.

### Relevance to this corpus
Shows a concrete planning choice that reallocates who must prove conflict, and when knowledge is demanded.
