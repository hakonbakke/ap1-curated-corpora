The article by Ingunn Elise Myklebust examines the challenges faced by Norwegian municipalities in managing land use in coastal waters under the Norwegian Plan and Building Act of 2008. The Act extends municipal planning authority to coastal waters, but the central government retains significant control through sector laws. Municipalities have the authority to make binding decisions regarding land-use plans, including coastal waters, but sector laws often place decision-making power with public sector authorities. Coastal waters are open for various activities, leading to competition for areas, and municipal plans are key tools for resolving conflicts of interest. The Planning and Building Act aims to strengthen cross-sectoral management, but municipalities face challenges in balancing local autonomy with national and regional interests. Municipalities have broad powers in land use planning, but these are constrained by coordination requirements and state control mechanisms. The Aquaculture Act requires that municipal land use plans allow for aquaculture, but municipalities cannot impose additional conditions for aquaculture establishment. The article highlights the importance of effective coordination between central, regional, and municipal authorities for successful coastal management. It also discusses the uncertainty about the extent of municipal autonomy in coastal planning due to overlapping responsibilities with central government authorities and the predictability of what constitutes 'national interests' in coastal management.
