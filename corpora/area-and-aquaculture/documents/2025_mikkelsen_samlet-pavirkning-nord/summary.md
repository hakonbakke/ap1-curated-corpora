### Background
Mikkelsen and colleagues report from the CLEAN project on how cumulative environmental impact is required and handled in northern Norway. Human activities, climate change, and pressures from outside the region can add up. The authors say reliable assessment is needed and is hard, because stressors interact and management is split by sector.

The report has two parts. First, a mapping of laws, regulations, and management plans for five cases: Svalbard, the Barents Sea and Lofoten waters, Nord-Norge coasts and fjords, terrestrial Nord-Norge, and the Pasvik watercourse. Second, overall results from a late-2024 survey of practice.

### Methods
The introduction sets two meanings of cumulative impact. One is several stressors on one nature object. The other is one activity with many pressures. Aquaculture is the example of the second (nutrients, lice, escapees, lice medicines, light, sound), citing Evenset et al. (2023). A new farm is treated as an extra load on already used water. The DAPSIR frame (drivers, activities, pressures, state, impact, response) is used to keep activities separate from drivers.

Work-package experts filled a table of relevant laws and plans, then scored each document on six question groups (object of management, holistic or ecosystem language, listed stressors, explicit cumulative duty, method requirements, and how management should respond). Some formally applicable laws were dropped as low practical relevance. Appendix Table 4 is the English coding template. Filled sheets are not in this extract.

The survey was built from earlier cumulative-impact surveys and project expertise. It was sent in November 2024 to 370 people (lowest-level managers in Nord-Norge municipalities and counties, plus directorates, ministries, and consultancies), with a request to forward. Respondents had two weeks and two reminders. Personal-data handling was assessed by Sikt (reference 501313).

### Main findings
Naturmangfoldloven section 10 and the impact-assessment regulation require cumulative load or combined effects. Section 10 is a guideline. Other considerations may still decide a case, but the assessment must be written down. Sivilombudet is cited: a missing section 7 assessment can be a procedural error. Many sector laws do not say cumulative. Of 18 laws and regulations, 6 use cumulative-impact or ecosystem-based wording. Another 6 use looser terms such as holistic or sustainable. Management plans almost all mention one of those ideas. Across cases, documents rarely say how the assessment should be done.

For coasts and fjords the authors point to municipal coastal planning (Planning and Building Act) and water management as the clearer holistic instruments. The water regulation scores indicators one-out-all-out and does not model stressor interaction. It gives no extra order or incentive power. The Aquaculture Act requires environmentally sound production and a holistic weighing of area needs and interests, including interests inside and outside aquaculture. The authors say it still has no explicit duty to assess cumulative impact, and no practical method guidance.

Barents Sea management plans are described as holistic and ecosystem-based, but not legally binding. Sectors remain free to interpret their own contribution. Nature Diversity Act applies only in part beyond 12 nautical miles. On Svalbard, Nature Diversity Act chapter II does not apply. Svalbard environmental law section 8 is the explicit cumulative-load rule in that case. That law was amended from 1 January 2025 to limit traffic pressure.

Norwegian KU practice investigates environment and society together. Evaluations cited (Multiconsult 2021, Pedersen et al. 2019) say economic and social themes often dominate, with weak environment chapters.

Seventy-six people answered (state 33, municipality 27, county 8, consultants 8). Forty-six percent said they assess cumulative impact to a large or very large degree. The closing chapter writes 45 percent. Including medium answers, the closing chapter gives 78 percent. Last cases split between one project and several activities. Most used discretion for stressor interactions (72 percent). Fifty-eight percent said they use guides for cumulative assessment. Sixty-three percent reported medium, low, or very little knowledge of how stressors interact. Data gaps were more often a medium or smaller problem than interaction-knowledge gaps.

### Uncertainty and limitations
The survey chapter is a rough overview. The authors say deeper analysis and interviews will follow. Seventy-six of 370 is not a probability sample. How discretion works, and how the precautionary principle is used, is not studied here. Several percentages sit in figures that are images in the extract. Filled legal-coding sheets are available from the authors, not here.

### Relevance to this corpus
This is the northern-law account of samlet belastning. For aquaculture it states what the Aquaculture Act does and does not require when a new farm adds load to an already used coast. Tag A3 and A4.
