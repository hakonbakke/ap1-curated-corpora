### Background
This 29-page SINTEF delrapport (2025:01117, version 1.3, 31 October 2025) is the aquaculture area description in the Trøndelag bioeconomy assignment. Sand and Misund write from the industry's perspective. Goals are to describe potential, the largest constraints and conflicts, and available area and production potential.

### Methods
Sources named in the introduction include informants from NCE Aquatech, a person with long Trøndelag aquaculture experience, and Sjømat Norge Midt, plus maps and statistics from Barentswatch, the Fisheries Directorate and SSB, and cited political and research documents. There is no separate sammendrag. Chapter 7 restates the three goals. Chapter 6 is a structured review of conflict types, not a new conflict census.

### Main findings
Atlantic salmon in open pens dominates. Physical occupation of production area in the sea is first stated as less than one percent of Trøndelag coastal zones. Citing Skrove et al. (2023), salmon farms are calculated at 15-42 km2 (0.2-0.4 percent), or about 0.5 percent with other species and a 100 m fishing ban. Chapter 7 restates a half percent. The same source, with 2.5 km and 1.5 km radii, is used to say farming takes about 20 percent of sea in production areas 6 and 7. That figure is called a coarse pointer.

Trøndelag holds about 19 percent of national grow-out capacity. Seafood was 61 percent of regional exports in 2022. In 2023 about 243 100 tonnes were slaughtered, first-hand value 17.2 billion kroner, almost all salmon. Chapter 4.1 estimates 11 400 jobs and 17.7 billion kroner value creation including ripples in 2022. Chapter 7 restates about 16 billion kroner. Municipal plans cover 10 856 km2 of coastal-zone sea. Municipalities have set aside 4 650 km2 (43 percent) to combined purposes that can include aquaculture. Osen has 10 km2 set aside for aquaculture alone, but applications there have often been refused because the municipality sits at the top of a production area and a sanitary zone.

Sites cluster around Hitra/Frøya and Nærøysund. A southern inter-municipal coastal plan exists from 2015. Namdalen and Fosen failed to make one. Company capacity in production areas 6 and 7 was 205 000 tonnes at the end of 2023, while cleared locality capacity exceeded 870 000 tonnes (79 percent in Trøndelag). Trøndelag is described as a yellow traffic-light zone.

Main constraints on today's system are lice, disease and escapes, weaker acceptance, slow public planning, and coastal power shortages. Submersible and more closed systems are under investment. Land-based grow-out could add volume if permits and power allow. A 2022 halt on land-based permits is said to have been lifted from summer 2025. Ten plants like the planned Kvidul site are said to match today's county salmon output. More than 10-20 such plants is called unrealistic. National salmon fjords still ban salmon grow-out.

Chapter 6 lists conflicts inside the sector (salmon versus cod, recommended 5 km distances, PD zones), with wild salmon and coastal cod (HI advice of very cautious cod-farm growth until 2025/2026 knowledge), kelp (about 1.2 km2 today, 82 km2 potential after conflict areas are removed), land-based irreversible works, offshore farming at Frøyabanken Nord and Trænabanken, feed-crop competition with agriculture, and visual, noise, defence and marine-protection interests.

### Uncertainty and limitations
Sanitary-distance percentages do not map suitability or other users. Land-based and offshore potential is qualitative. Value-creation figures differ among first-hand 2023 value, the 2022 ripple estimate, and the chapter 7 restatement.

### Relevance to this corpus
It applies the national physical-versus-sanitary area argument to one large production county, and it names the conflict types that sit behind those percentages.
