This study examines local perceptions of the aquaculture industry in Northern Norway, focusing on its legitimacy and impact on coastal communities. The research highlights a legitimacy gap between the blue growth goals of the Norwegian government and fishers, who perceive aquaculture negatively due to restricted access to areas, declining shrimp catches, and changes in fish quality. In contrast, the general public holds a more positive view, appreciating the socio-economic benefits of aquaculture, such as employment and increased municipal income. The study emphasizes the importance of differentiating between the general public and key stakeholders like fishers in assessing aquaculture's legitimacy. It suggests that improving input legitimacy for key stakeholders could enhance the industry's overall legitimacy. The research relies on a mix of qualitative and quantitative methods, including structured and semi-structured interviews, to gather data from two coastal communities in Northern Norway. The findings underscore the need for a nuanced understanding of local perceptions to develop a socially acceptable and sustainable aquaculture industry. The study's insights are valuable for local, regional, and national decision-makers responsible for natural resource policies and development efforts.
