### Background
Aldrin, Huseby and Jansen model pancreas disease (PD), caused by salmonid alphavirus, within and between Norwegian marine farms producing Atlantic salmon and rainbow trout. The aims stated in the paper are to describe space-time PD dynamics and to use the fitted model for scenario simulation of control measures. Municipal coastal planning and site designation are not part of the analysis.

### Methods
The model is hierarchical and Bayesian, in continuous time, estimated with MCMC and latent infection times. Between-farm infection has four additive pathways: seaway-distance neighbourhood spread, the same local contact network (farms sharing a concession identity), a previous infected cohort at the same farm (within six months), and other non-specified sources. Within-farm infection follows an SIR process with a seasonal transmission rate. Infection and detected outbreak are separate processes, so a cohort can be infected without a recorded outbreak.

Data cover all 1412 Norwegian marine salmonid farms active in any month from February 2003 to February 2012, 4332 cohorts, 62 408 farm-months, monthly fish numbers and weights, seawater temperature, seaway distances truncated at 100 km, and 588 detected PD outbreaks. 645 cohorts had unknown stocking times and 519 had unknown removal times. 73 percent of cohorts were smolts. A ten-fold cross-validation compared six SIR transmission-rate variants. The selected model uses two pairs of sine and cosine seasonal terms plus fish numbers and weights in susceptibility and infectiousness, and has 23 parameters.

### Main findings
Farms had on average 6.3 other farms within 10 km seaway and 1.2 within 3 km. Monthly active farms ranged from 471 to 706. Mean fish number was 785 000. Mean seawater temperature was 8.9 degrees C. 96 percent of detected outbreaks were south of Hustadvika (62 degrees 57 minutes N), while 46 percent of cohorts were farmed in that area.

Neighbourhood infection related to seaway distance accounts for 87.8 percent of infection events (credible interval 81.1-94.2 percent). The contact network accounts for 5.6 percent, previous cohorts 2.8 percent, other sources 2.3 percent, and infection already present at stocking about 1.5 percent. About 20.3 percent of cohorts were estimated infected, against detected outbreaks in 13.6 percent of cohorts. About one third of infected cohorts had no detected outbreak. Mean delay from infection to detection was 3.6 months among detected cases. Risk of infection from neighbourhood farms falls sharply as seaway distance increases (shown as a decay curve in the paper).

A scenario that removes cohorts within a month of a detected outbreak on all farms, compared with a pattern like the northern cull rule versus unrestricted southern practice, reduced detected outbreaks by 57 percent (interval 24-90 percent) over the last five simulated years, or 42 outbreaks per year (18-69), at a cost of 174 farm-months of production per year (interval 4-331). The discussion rounds that reduction as approximately 60 percent.

### Uncertainty and limitations
The model predicted whether a cohort would have an outbreak much better than a naive baseline, but simulated outbreak maps were less clustered than the observations. The authors suggest extra unknown factors, including raised diagnostic awareness after a nearby PD diagnosis. The other-source term is estimated from endemic data and may not describe introduction into a disease-free area. Vaccination rose during the period and is not identified by cohort. Re-location of farms is named as a possible scenario type and is not run. This is not a study of coastal-zone plans or licence geography.

### Relevance to this corpus
Adjacent. Seaway distance, neighbourhood farms, and local farm density (counts within 3 km and 10 km) are explicit. A5 is tagged for that biosecurity geography. The paper is still a PD control model, not a planning paper.

### Relation to other documents
Extends the authors' earlier ISA and multi-disease models, including Aldrin et al. (2011) on ISA seaway and genetic distance. The 2017 lice farm model is a related farm-level parasite model, not a PD sequel.
