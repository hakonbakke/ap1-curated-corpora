### Background
Havforskningsinstituttet's Risikorapport norsk fiskeoppdrett 2026 is the official annual risk report on production mortality in farmed fish and environmental effects of Norwegian fish farming (Rapport fra havforskningen 2026-10, 76 pages, 11 February 2026). It scores nine unwanted events that can reduce sustainability with respect to welfare and environment in a production area. Few conclusions changed from 2025, so this volume prints only the 13 area summaries. Coastal-zone planning and municipal designation of sea area are not the main object.

### Methods
The report combines monitoring, models, official statistics, and expert judgement (Andersen et al., 2022). Probability, knowledge strength, and overall risk are shown in a summary figure for each of the 13 production areas. The authors state that there are no objective risk numbers. Welfare uses total loss (dead, discard, escape, other, excluding count error) because loss categories are used differently across areas. About 17 percent year-class loss is treated as moderate. Lice scores use the same bands as the traffic-light system (under 10 percent, 10-30 percent, over 30 percent). Table 1.1 colour cells and the 13 risk-map figures are images. Per-area lice scores in this note come from chapter 3 prose. Coastal-cod risk notes are published separately. A climate-focused assessment is planned before summer 2026.

### Main findings
Production rose by about 200,000 tonnes from 2024 to 2025, the largest annual rise since 1980. Table 1.2 gives 1,838,665 tonnes of grow-out production in 2025. Salmon accounted for 182,000 tonnes. Cod slaughter was 20,130 tonnes. Growth was greatest from Nord-Trøndelag to Loppa. Mean monthly standing salmon fell from 459 million in 2024 to 445 million in 2025.

Two-year means from 2018-2019 to 2024-2025 rose in every production area except area 1. Red areas 3 and 4 still added about 67,000 tonnes. Yellow areas 2 and 5-8 added about 243,000 tonnes. Green areas 1 and 9-13 added about 135,500 tonnes. Area 4, red since the first traffic-light round and cut three times, had the second-largest rise (about 42,000 tonnes). The 2024 traffic-light decision cut areas 3 and 4, held 2 and 5-8 flat, and allowed growth in 9-13. The government text spoke of about 21,000 tonnes of capacity growth. Salmonid production still rose by just over 197,000 tonnes, including in red and yellow areas, via higher utilisation, exception growth, cross-area company MTB, and larger post-smolt. The authors ask whether that rise is environmentally sustainable given lice pressure, especially for sea trout.

Poor farmed-fish welfare and lice effects on wild fish are stated as the main sustainability obstacles. Directorate of Fisheries figures: 67 million farmed salmon lost in 2025, of which more than 54 million died in cages. That is three million fewer dead fish than in 2024. National year-class loss stays at about 16-18 percent. High welfare risk is stated for seven of 13 areas (1-6 and 12). Only area 8 is low. Rainbow-trout welfare risk rose from moderate to high in area 3. Area 12 is described as heading toward about 25 percent loss per year-class. Non-medicinal delousing was 47 percent of welfare incident reports in 2025. Area 6 had the most such cases.

Lice-induced mortality in wild salmon smolts: high in areas 3 and 4, moderate in 2 and 5-11, low in 1, 12 and 13. For sea trout and Arctic char the summary states nine high, two moderate, two low, with high risk from Karmøy to Senja (areas 2-10). Submerged cages rose from about 10 sites in 2023 to about 40 in 2025, in areas 3-9 and 11. More than 30 million salmon have been harvested from submerged cages. Open cages, the amount of farmed salmonids, and tight infection coupling between sites are said to make low lice levels very hard to keep. A 2025 rule lets reduced traffic-light capacity be used in closed units if documentation is accepted.

Genetic-introgression risk remains high in four areas (3, 6, 7 and 8). Area 6 is raised from last year. Area 9 is lowered. Nutrient emissions are low risk in all areas. Estimated 2025 dissolved releases are 70,665 tonnes nitrogen and 9,377 tonnes phosphorus. Areas 2 and 3 have the highest release per sea area. Local model pockets can reach 30-50 percent phytoplankton increase. Seabed risk is largely unchanged. Area 4 is moved from moderate to low. National B-surveys: 9.8 percent poor or very poor. Mean influence area is 4 percent of production-area sea. Area 3 influence is about 15 percent. Copper use rose from 306 tonnes in 2023 to 564 tonnes in 2024, still far below 2019. Current delousing-chemical use is assessed as low risk. Wild wrasse catch fell to about 5.4 million in 2025. 2026 is the last year with a separate cleaner-fish score if harvest stays low.

### Uncertainty and limitations
The authors state that little is known about carrying capacity for environmental effects in each production area. Knowledge of future marine heatwaves and lice is described as limited. Nutrient models sometimes show risk that measurements do not support. Hard-bottom methods are incomplete. Smolt migration routes are poorly known in several areas. Welfare scores are relative, not ethical. Areas with few sites are sensitive to single events. Table 1.1 colours and the risk-map figures are not readable as cell values. This is not a study of municipal coastal plans or site applications.

### Relevance to this corpus
Adjacent. Production areas, traffic-light capacity, site counts, more fish in the sea, and infection coupling touch density and siting (A5). Wild-fish and environmental trade-offs are scored by area (A3). National fish-farming risk is still the object.
