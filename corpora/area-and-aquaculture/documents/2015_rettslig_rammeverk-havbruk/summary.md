### Background
Solås and co-authors map the legal and administrative framework for Norwegian aquaculture as the first report from FHF project 900902 on effects of the legal framework and possible simplification. Political growth aims (environmentally sustainable growth in Meld. St. 16, 2014-2015) are distinguished from the administrative organisation and tools that actually shape growth. The project had three work packages. This report is the status map. Practice and conflict packages were still to come.

### Methods
The work is based on statutes, preparatory works, guidance, reports, letters and decisions, scientific articles and related earlier projects. An industry reference group commented on direction and on a draft. The theoretical frame treats management as interaction between a management system and a system to be managed (Solås and Johnsen 2014, after Johnsen 2014). There is no new statistical measurement of processing times in the extract. Chapter 4 is a framework for static and dynamic costs of regulation, not a measured cost account.

### Main findings
The system is described as sectorised, fragmented and complex. Aquaculture Act section 6 gives Mattilsynet, pollution authorities, Kystverket and (for freshwater) NVE an effective veto on site clearance. Section 15 lets municipalities close sea area through plans. No authority is assigned to weigh all interests for and against a site. A refusal on one sector ground can stop a site even if a joint view would allow it. County governors' nature advice under the Nature Diversity Act (including cumulative load in section 10) is not a veto. How often counties follow that advice is left unknown.

Two governance systems meet: instrumental state sector administration (for example lice rules) and negotiated municipal area planning. Poor fit between them is named as a barrier to better site use and site structure. After the 2010 reform, locality decisions sit with the counties. The Trøndelag model (2002-2009) that transferred decision authority to the Fisheries Directorate is said not to have been repeated. County aquaculture units are said to have one to three officers. Informal coordination exists. Effect on decisions is not measured.

Impact-assessment rules do not require inclusion of aquaculture's positive effects. Relevant guidance for aquaculture impact assessment is described as missing. The 2015 KU revision is said to drop the old 3 600 MTB size trigger and to follow EU influence rather than farm size. Small pen or site adjustments still require a full site application. Applications are described as 75 to 150 pages, against a two-page form in the mid-1980s.

Physical occupation including moorings is given as 420 km2, or less than five per thousand of the area inside the baseline (Andreassen et al., 2010). Approved sites fell from 1 806 in 2000 to 946 in 2015, with about 550 to 600 in use. Sites above 3 600 MTB rose from 2 percent in 2006 to 30 percent in January 2015. Recommended Food Safety Authority distances are 2.5 km, or 5 km firebreaks, from guidance rather than statute. A Directorate for Nature Management letter (2009) asking counties to treat 30 km from salmon rivers carefully is said to leave almost no usable area in Troms for then-current technology.

Many municipalities have been reluctant to free more area, citing low local return. NFKK has pressed for a production or area fee. Government instead steered parts of new-licence fees toward counties (40 percent in 2009) or host municipalities (40 percent in 2013). Green licences in 2014 are stated to send 480 million kroner to municipalities (15 auctioned at 55-65 million, 30 at 10 million). The 45 licences are estimated at about 55 000 tonnes, or about 5 percent of 2014 production. The authors list weaknesses of one-off fee shares: irregular rounds, no payment for existing sites, problems for fire-break municipalities, and room for hidden bargaining. Hersoug et al. (2014) are cited: 87 percent of farmers agreed that a fixed annual municipal payment would ease access to new area. A company survey in this report says 63 percent agree that access to deep, well-flushed, sheltered sites is the main challenge, and 37 percent name area access as the largest growth constraint.

The 2015 white-paper traffic-light proposal is described as still unresolved, earliest 2017. If licence MTB grows, locality MTB may become the binding limit. Directorate of Fisheries is quoted as saying production areas would be hard to run with today's fragmented administration. The authors sketch an objection-like path to the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Fisheries after a sector refusal, and a simplification that would clear an area inside which the farmer chooses exact placement.

### Uncertainty and limitations
Severity and cost of negative externalities are stated as debated and not estimated here. The authors say optimal regulation is not zero escapees. How far informal county coordination changes decisions is not measured. National salmon-fjord practice is illustrated, not counted. The report is a status map, not a completed simplification package.
