This study explores the role of informal, self-organized networks in Norwegian aquaculture, focusing on horizontal collaborations among salmon entrepreneurs and vertical collaborations between companies and municipalities. It highlights how these networks contribute to sustainability by fostering mutual learning, adaptability, and resilience. Horizontal networks excel in knowledge sharing and collective action, addressing biological risks and economic challenges. Vertical networks emphasize public-private collaboration, driving community and economic development, but show limited engagement with environmental sustainability. The study underscores the complementary strengths of these networks and their role in enhancing resilience and sustainability in aquaculture. However, regulatory constraints and relational dependencies pose challenges to proactive environmental collaboration. The findings align with adaptive co-management principles, demonstrating the importance of trust, local knowledge, and iterative adaptation. The study employs a qualitative methodology, drawing on 20 semi-structured interviews and policy document analysis from two regions in Northern Norway. It provides insights into how informal governance systems can enhance resilience and sustainability in aquaculture, with broader implications for resource-dependent industries navigating complex socio-ecological landscapes.
