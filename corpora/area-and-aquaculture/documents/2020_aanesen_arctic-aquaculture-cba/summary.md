The study by Aanesen and Mikkelsen (2020) provides a cost-benefit analysis of aquaculture expansion in Arctic Norway, focusing on five municipalities. It highlights the importance of producer surplus remaining in the region for a positive net present value (NPV) of aquaculture expansion. The analysis considers the benefits of increased aquaculture production, including consumer and producer surplus and transfers from the national Aquaculture Fund. Costs are assessed through opportunity costs of land use, measured by households' willingness to pay to avoid aquaculture expansion. The study identifies uncertainties in estimating producer surplus, payments from the Aquaculture Fund, and households' willingness to pay to avoid aquaculture. It suggests that transfers from the Aquaculture Fund are often insufficient to outweigh regional households' welfare loss from more aquaculture activities. The analysis assumes that aquaculture expansion is marginal to the regional economy and does not affect prices or economic growth. The study emphasizes the need for some producer surplus to remain in the region to ensure positive regional NPV. Methodological limitations include the lack of knowledge on environmental damages from aquaculture and the survey's willingness to pay estimates not being statistically significant at a 5% level. The research was funded by the Research Council of Norway and supported by Horizon 2020 research and innovation program.
