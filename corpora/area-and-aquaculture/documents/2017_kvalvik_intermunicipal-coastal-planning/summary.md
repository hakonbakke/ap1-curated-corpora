### Background
Kvalvik and Robertsen examine whether inter-municipal coastal zone planning is a tool for more coordinated planning in Norway, in particular for designation of aquaculture area. Municipalities have designated sea area under the Planning and Building Act since 1989. Several municipalities usually share one fjord, while veterinary distance rules and other sea uses cross those borders.

### Methods
The study covers all nine inter-municipal coastal zone planning processes up to October 2016 (65 municipalities in six counties). It uses planning documents, planning programmes and interim reports, interviews with the leaders of the processes, and participation at regional meetings on aquaculture and area access with companies, regional state authorities and county councils. Integration is coded as policy cooperation, policy coordination, or policy integration of content.

### Main findings
Production of salmon more than doubled from 600 000 tonnes in 2000 to more than 1.2 million tonnes in 2014, while site numbers fell from about 1800 to about 1000 and production per site rose from 278 to 1303 tonnes. Occupied area including safety and anchoring is given as 0.5 percent of coastal area inside the baseline. Production took place in 170 of 276 coastal municipalities. Municipal willingness to designate new area is described as critical, alongside industry-internal limits (2.5 km between farms, 5 km to a slaughtery, proposed production zones). The last four years before the paper there had been no production growth.

All nine processes showed a high degree of dialogue and of joint organisation, shared data collection and similar planning tools. Seven boards were delegated process authority. Helgeland and Tromsø were not. Full coordination of plan content, including aquaculture designation for the whole sea area, was found in four processes with two to five municipalities (Lyngenfjord, Fensfjord, Romsdalsfjord, Nord-Troms), 15 of the 65 municipalities. Fensfjord jointly chose not to designate new aquaculture area. That reduces the count with aquaculture clarification to 10. Helgeland used inverse planning and, as of October 2016, had been stopped at county level after sector protests. Sør-Trøndelag stopped at regional guidelines without a joint plan map and set areas shallower than 25 m as non-aquaculture. Navy sequestration constrained several processes. In South and Mid Troms and Nordmøre, aquaculture designation often reverted to each municipality when interests diverged. Nordmøre did jointly protect salmon-river areas and mapped no-transport and no-fishing buffers that crossed borders. The Tromsø region collected a joint wish list but did not coordinate the map. On a shared national salmon fjord, Tromsø designated no aquaculture while South and Mid Troms designated the south side. Only Fensfjorden had formal post-plan cooperation procedures.

The authors conclude that inter-municipal planning remedies some, not all, piece-by-piece municipal planning, and that this first generation of plans is still a better tool than single-municipality planning. They found more content integration than expected, but only in small processes.

### Uncertainty and limitations
Day-to-day management after adoption is not measured. Whether later revisions stay inter-municipal is left open. Integration scores rest on the authors' reading of nine processes and process-leader interviews, not on later site-licence outcomes.

### Relevance to this corpus
This is a core text on whether voluntary inter-municipal plans can coordinate aquaculture designation when each council keeps the final say.
