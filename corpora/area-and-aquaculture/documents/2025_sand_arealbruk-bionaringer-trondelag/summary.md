### Background
Trøndelag County Municipality commissioned a knowledge base on area use, area need, conflicts and political dilemmas around five bio-based industries: fisheries, aquaculture, agriculture, forestry and reindeer husbandry. SINTEF report 2025:00943 (version 1.1, 31 October 2025, 52 pages plus annexes) is the main report. The project ran from November 2024 to October 2025.

### Methods
The authors built a new cross-sector frame because no established method existed. Data include key-informant interviews (Bondelaget, WoodWorks!, Norske Reindriftssamers Landsforbund, Fiskarlaget, NCE Aquatech, Sjømat Norge, and county offices), registers, maps, documents and statistics. Sector notes were sent to industry representatives for comment. SINTEF added its own time beyond the funded contract. Analyses and conclusions are the authors' responsibility. Views of actors outside the five industries rest on earlier research, not new interviews with those opponents.

### Main findings
All five industries depend on suitable production area, not only a measured surface. Prior mapping of suitability beyond area and biological productivity is uneven. Table 1 estimates the five industries plus processing and ripples at 40.1 billion kroner, or 15.0 percent of regional value creation (268.2 billion kroner in 2022 county accounts), and 34 480 jobs. Aquaculture is the largest value-creation sector in that table (18.2 billion kroner, 11 400 jobs). Agriculture is the largest employer. The aquaculture digest in section 3.2 instead uses about 16 billion kroner, which is the companion delrapport's chapter 7 figure.

Table 2 states today's pattern: intensive exclusive aquaculture on 0.5 percent (50 km2) of the coastal zone, intensive exclusive agriculture on 4 percent of land, forestry on about 9 percent, reindeer extensive use on about 70 percent of land, and fisheries extensive use of almost all sea. Informants in every sector worry about future access, mainly against other public purposes.

On land, the largest fights over productive bio-industry area are said to come from outside the five sectors: buildings, infrastructure and nature conservation. At sea the same pattern includes petroleum, energy, shipping, tourism and nature. Fisheries and aquaculture also disagree on how precautionary one should be about aquaculture's effect on marine ecosystems.

Chapter 4 says regional aquaculture growth is stopped first by environmental and fish-health problems in today's systems, not by a shortage of pen area or regional feed. The authors repeat that about 20 percent of sea in production areas 6 and 7 is occupied once sanitary distances are included (Skrove et al. 2023). Named growth ambitions are a fivefold national aquaculture aim, a doubling of regional timber harvest, about 20 percent more food from agriculture, maintain-or-slightly-increase for fisheries, and maintain for reindeer.

The authors want area work built on the county bioeconomy coordination group if all five industries are active there. Reindeer husbandry is named as missing from that group. They warn against treating working bio-production as the same thing as permanently lost built nature. Regional plan guideline R28 asks for enough sea for expected aquaculture growth, preferably through inter-municipal plans. Chapter 5.5 treats social licence, including Osen as a lice and disease buffer with little compensation.

### Uncertainty and limitations
The method is new. Informants disagreed on themes and tone. Table 1 figures are 2023-level estimates. Main-report aquaculture numbers mix a short digest of annex 2 with that table. Annexes 1 to 5 are listed, not reproduced here.

### Relevance to this corpus
It sets aquaculture area in Trøndelag inside a five-industry bioeconomy, which is the county's own planning frame. Chapters 4 and 5 state the trade-offs and the knowledge gaps.

### Relation to other documents
Must be read with Sand and Misund 2025 (Havbrukets arealbehov i Trøndelag), which is annex 2. Cites Skrove et al. 2023 (SALT 1075).
