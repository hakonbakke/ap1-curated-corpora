The study explores the societal support for aquaculture production in Norway, focusing on the impact of economic and social factors. It finds that people living in municipalities with aquaculture activities have a more favorable view of the industry, but there is a perception that economic benefits are not fairly shared. The research highlights the importance of proximity to aquaculture activities in improving social acceptance and the role of perceptions of equitable distribution of economic benefits. The study uses survey data combined with municipality-level data on production and economic footprint, employing ordered logistic regression models to analyze relationships between perceptions and municipality characteristics. The findings suggest that increasing coastal communities' share of benefits could enhance the industry's legitimacy and that stronger incentives for municipalities to prioritize aquaculture sites could facilitate access to better production areas. The study also notes that the aquaculture industry in Norway has evolved significantly, with increased production and economic significance for coastal communities. However, societal expectations regarding the distribution of benefits and adherence to regulations remain unmet, indicating a need for improved societal acceptance to facilitate industry growth. The study provides valuable insights into the factors affecting societal support for aquaculture, emphasizing the importance of equitable benefit distribution and the role of proximity to aquaculture activities.
