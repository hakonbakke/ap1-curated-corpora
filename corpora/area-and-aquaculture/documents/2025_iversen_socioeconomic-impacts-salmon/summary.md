This review article explores the socioeconomic impacts of the Norwegian salmon aquaculture industry, focusing on employment and value creation. The industry is a significant contributor to the Norwegian economy, supporting 57,800 jobs, including 38,000 from ripple effects. These ripple effects extend beyond core production activities, highlighting the industry's importance to local and national economies. The analysis reveals that aquaculture production is concentrated along the Norwegian coast, with notable regional differences in production growth. The industry is highly profitable, contributing disproportionately to Norwegian tax revenues compared to its GDP share. However, the reliance on imported feed ingredients limits domestic ripple effects, despite the industry's overall economic impact. The study employs input-output analysis to assess the industry's impact, but acknowledges limitations due to data availability and the exclusion of international ripple effects. The findings underscore the importance of considering both direct and indirect effects when evaluating the socioeconomic contributions of aquaculture. The study also highlights the role of the Aquaculture Fund in distributing income from the industry to local municipalities, promoting regional development. Overall, the article provides valuable insights into the complex interactions between aquaculture and socioeconomic factors in Norway.
