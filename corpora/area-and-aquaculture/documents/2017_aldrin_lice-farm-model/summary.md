### Background
Aldrin, Huseby, Stien, Grøntvedt, Viljugrein and Jansen estimate a stage-structured population model for salmon lice (Lepeophtheirus salmonis) at individual salmon farms. The stated purpose is to capture farm hierarchy, temperature-dependent rates, fish size and abundance effects, control measures, and distance-based transmission between farms. The paper is a farm parasite model. It is not a study of coastal-zone planning or municipal site access.

The introduction cites earlier work that large-scale host density dependence has been shown for parasites and viruses in salmon farming. Those density statements are citations, not new national density estimates here. Norway’s production is given as about 1.2 million tonnes, roughly half of worldwide production, citing Anonymous (2015).

### Methods
The model is fully mechanistic and Bayesian hierarchical. Parameters are estimated from cage-level production and lice-count data on 32 marine salmon farms. Twelve of those farms sit just north of Frøya. Salmon were stocked between 2011 and 2013 and slaughtered about one and a half years later, between 2012 and 2014. Cages per farm ranged from 3 to 12 (mean 7.7). Mean seawater temperature at 3 m was 9.1 degrees C (95 percent of readings between 3.6 and 15.0). Salinity was unused because the authors say it was unavailable in sufficient detail. The last 3-11 months of data for five farms were held out for prediction checks.

External recruitment is a seaway-distance-weighted sum of adult female lice at neighbouring farms. The distance function g(d) = exp(-0.618 d^0.568) is taken from Aldrin et al. (2013), estimated there from more than eight years of data on about 1400 farms, and is not re-estimated here. Neighbouring adult females are assumed to be at stage-age 10. Marine Harvest, Salmar and Måsøval supplied the farm data. Funding is FHF project 900970.

### Main findings
Expected values fitted observed infection levels of chalimus, adult female, and other mobile stages reasonably well. Predictions for the held-out months were consistent with the observational data.

The average share of recruitment that is internal (from lice already on the farm) is 25 percent (credible interval 23-26 percent), with median 19 percent and a farm range of about 4 to 73 percent. Internal recruitment was more important when adult-female abundance was high, and especially on farms without neighbouring farms. Sixteen of the 32 farms had at least one neighbour within 3.54 km seaway. The authors say this internal share is below Aldrin et al. (2013) and closer to a median of 18 percent in Adlandsvik (2015), a hydrodynamic simulation of 591 farms.

At 10 degrees C, ten days after bath treatment, expected cumulative mortality in pre-adult or adult stages was 0.99 for hydrogen peroxide, 0.94 for deltamethrin or cypermethrin, and 0.75 for azamethiphos. The authors report no evidence of an Allee effect on reproduction. Daily cleaner-fish mortality is 0.028 (interval 0.022-0.034), implying about a one-month half-life. At a 10 percent cleaner-fish to salmon ratio, daily lice mortality from cleaner fish is 0.079 (0.060-0.099), and adult-lice life expectancy falls from 8.2 to 5.2 days. The authors present this as a first data-based quantification of cleaner-fish effect in full-scale farms.

### Uncertainty and limitations
The between-farm distance kernel is inherited. The sample is 32 farms, not all Norwegian sites. The assumed stage-age of neighbouring adult females is described as arbitrary, with a claim that results were insensitive to that choice. Treatment effects are hard to compare because duration and resistance vary. Coastal plans, area rent, and licence geography are not analysed.

### Relevance to this corpus
Adjacent. The extract uses seaway distances and neighbouring farms to build external infection pressure. The object remains lice population dynamics at the farm. A5 is tagged because neighbourhood distance and farm-level recruitment speak to density and biosecurity. This is not a siting paper.

### Relation to other documents
Related in method family to Aldrin et al. (2011) on ISA and Aldrin et al. (2015) on PD, which also use seaway or neighbourhood terms. The lice kernel itself is cited from Aldrin et al. (2013), which is not a document in this room. Adlandsvik (2015) is used only as an 18 percent comparison, not as a planning analysis here.
