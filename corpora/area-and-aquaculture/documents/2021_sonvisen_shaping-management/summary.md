Sønvisen and Vik (2021) argue that Norwegian salmon farming has grown under environmental limits, and that the traffic-light system was introduced to allow growth that is environmentally sustainable. Their claim is that the regime still leaves out legitimacy and acceptance, which they treat as necessary for the industry to develop.

The stated method is qualitative: interviews and document analysis. The abstract says stakeholders perceive and weigh the dimensions of sustainability differently. Low social sustainability, from low content legitimacy and acceptance, puts them in an interest tug of war. The stated conclusion is that weak legitimacy and acceptance can block the growth objective and can weaken procedural legitimacy and the institutional setup.

The introduction cites, rather than measures, a 2019 production of 1.1 million tonnes of Atlantic salmon, almost 9000 people employed in aquaculture, and an export value of about NOK 72.5 billion. It also cites a government ambition of a fivefold increase between 2010 and 2050, and an industry claim of 133,000 jobs and 17.5 billion meals. Those figures are context. They are not results of this study.

This note is drawn from the title, abstract and introduction. The interview material later in the paper was not walked for this draft.
