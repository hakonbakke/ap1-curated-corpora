### Background
Pettersen, Osmundsen, Aunsmo, Mardones and Rich review control of emerging infectious diseases in Atlantic salmon farming (Rev. Sci. Tech. Off. Int. Epiz. 2015, 34(3), 923-938). The cases are infectious salmon anaemia worldwide and pancreas disease in Norway. The paper uses lessons from terrestrial animal disease control and asks how transmission, reservoirs and institutions shape what can be done at sea.

### Methods
Narrative review. No new outbreak dataset is analysed in the paper. Maps of farms with pancreas disease are acknowledged as edited from Veterinary Institute material.

### Main findings
Production in semi-open net pens exchanges water with local currents. Waste and infectious agents move with that water. The risk of ISA and PD rises when an infected farm is close. An epidemiological unit here is a group of fish with about the same risk, such as one farm.

Control tools are chemical treatment, vaccination, breeding for resistance, and zoosanitary measures. Zoosanitary measures include fallowing, movement restrictions, surveillance, waste management and disinfection. They can be used even when the cause is poorly known.

ISA has been brought under control in Norway, the Faroe Islands and Scotland, where government took the main responsibility and public-private partnerships engaged the firms. In Chile, neighbourhood organisation and biosecurity improved after similar partnerships. PD in Norway has been less systematically controlled. The authors say technical options exist, and that cost-sharing with the private sector plus stronger public-private cooperation would help more than the current institutional setup. A single producer cannot close ISA or PD alone.

The decision context is described as uncertain, disagreed and politicised, with weak incentives for prompt reporting and a lack of effective joint control.

### Uncertainty and limitations
This is a review, not a transmission model. It does not estimate a distance at which infection stops. Institutional claims about PD governance are argued from the cases, not measured as a new survey.

### Relevance to this corpus
The paper answers how biosecurity, disease transmission and water contact between farms matter for production and operation. It does not analyse municipal plans or the locality application.
