### Background
Aldrin, Lyngstad, Kristoffersen, Storvik, Borgan and Jansen model how infectious salmon anaemia (ISA) spreads among Norwegian marine salmon farms. The object is the infection rate of a susceptible farm by a given ISA virus genotype. Seaway distance to infectious neighbourhood farms and genetic distance between isolates are the main spatial and phylogenetic terms. Coastal planning and municipal site designation are not analysed.

### Methods
The model is a stochastic continuous-time infection-rate model, fitted by maximum partial likelihood. It extends an earlier Aldrin et al. model by adding genetic distance. Infection is split into (i) neighbourhood farms and (ii) other non-specified pathways (examples given: well boats, infected smolts). Neighbourhood risk declines as seaway distance and genetic distance rise. Susceptibility and infectiousness can depend on cohort type and maximum number of fish.

Data are an update of earlier work: all 1201 Norwegian marine farms with Atlantic salmon in any month from February 2003 to August 2009, 3202 cohorts, monthly biomass and fish numbers, and seaway distances (shortest path over seawater, truncated at 100 km). ISA diagnoses come from the National Veterinary Institute laboratory system (72 diagnoses in the period, 63 from 2004). Those 63 diagnoses are the events in the partial likelihood. Four of them had unknown genotypes. The haemagglutinin-esterase gene was sequenced from all but six infected farms. Infection time is unobserved. The main fit assumes a six-month delay from infection to recorded diagnosis. Ten of 63 infection times were then set equal to stocking. Eight of those ten were relocated cohorts.

### Main findings
Seaway and genetic distance parameters were both positive and significant. The seaway estimate (f = 0.095) implies about a 60 percent drop in risk from a neighbourhood farm when distance rises from 0 to 10 km. The genetic estimate (v = 1.42) implies about a 75 percent drop when genetic distance rises from 0 to 1. Averaging over infected farms, about 43 percent of infections from January 2004 to August 2009 are attributed to neighbourhood infectious farms (Table 1 interval 40-64 percent). Including genes changed that share only from 47 to 43 percent, but made farm-level source probabilities more extreme. The earlier 2004-2007 neighbourhood share, without genes, was 22 percent.

Stocking category (autumn, mixed, relocated versus spring) was not significant. Susceptibility rose with cohort size. Infectiousness was marginally non-significant in the full model (p-value 0.06).

Most infected farms were isolated in time and space. A cluster in Troms after June 2007 is treated as neighbourhood-dominated: the model starts at one farm (ISA recorded June 2007) and traces spread to 16 other farms within two years, with identical genotypes on 15 of 17 farms. Some high-probability pathways exceed 20 km, and one southern example is about 30 km (probability 0.87). For the three pairs beyond 20 km, follow-up found no shared ownership or smolt source. Two pairs had shared a well boat and a dive team. The authors note that 5 km and 10 km are the restriction and observation zone radii then used by the Norwegian Food Safety Authority.

### Uncertainty and limitations
About half of infections are left as other sources. The delay from infection to diagnosis is fixed, not estimated. Hydrodynamic, directional distance was not available nationally. Multiple genotypes on one cohort are reduced to the smallest inter-farm genetic distance. Cohort movements could not be traced. The discussion once dates the neighbourhood share to January 2003 to August 2009, while the 43 percent figure uses January 2004 to August 2009. The paper does not evaluate coastal-zone plans, area rent, or licence geography.

### Relevance to this corpus
Adjacent. The extract uses seaway distance and neighbourhood farms, so it speaks to how close farms sit in the water (A5). It does not speak to who may designate sea area or how municipalities plan the coast.

### Relation to other documents
Pairs with Aldrin, Huseby and Jansen (2015) on pancreas disease space-time spread, which reuses seaway distance and adds a contact network. The 2017 lice farm model uses a distance kernel from related lice work, not this ISA fit.
