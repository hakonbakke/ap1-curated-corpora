### Background
Hersoug reviews why Norwegian aquaculture still runs on licences, how discretion relates to more objective criteria, and what special-purpose licences do to the main system. The title contrast is one country, ten systems.

### Methods
Historical and administrative review of licensing as a tool, Norwegian aquaculture history 1973-2013, then green licences and the traffic-light system, special-purpose schemes, development licences, and land-based, post-smolt and offshore schemes. Materials are prior research, white papers, award and rejection letters, 27 consultation responses on development licences, and interviews with the Directorate, the ministry, politicians and scheme operators.

### Main findings
A licence is twofold: production volume as MAB, and a locality right. Ordinary commercial growth is under the traffic-light system. Special-purpose licences are a separate family, often created after industry requests. Ten companies were responsible for about 70 percent of production.

Special-purpose licences support broodstock, education, research, exhibition and development. Hersoug also states they have been a backdoor to cheaper capacity. Table 1 (October 2020) gives 280 special-purpose licences and 207 836 tons MAB (21 percent of numbers, 17 percent of MAB) against 1057 commercial licences and 1 016 540 tons MAB. Ordinary commercial licences trade around USD 15 to 20 million (NOK 150 to 200 million) for 780 tons MAB. Special licences are not number-capped, can sit in a red area, and are not cut when an area turns red. After a development licence is converted (fixed NOK 10 million), there is no duty to keep the new technology.

Green licences (45, in three groups) were an expensive experiment later summed up as never again. The traffic-light system from 2017 uses one lice indicator. Thresholds (under 10, 10 to 30, over 30 percent chance of harm to wild salmon) are described as discretionary. A minister changed one area from yellow to green. Production area 4 farmers sued and lost. Red-area cuts were waived in the 2017 trial and applied from 2020. Development licences drew 104 applications for 892 standard units. Twenty companies received about 80 000 tons MAB. The investment-test licence value rose from about NOK 50 million to about NOK 150 million after June 2018.

Allocation is more objective than before, but political and administrative discretion remains in colouring, carrying-capacity figures, county ranking of competing sites, and innovation tests. Reform attempts have largely failed. Municipalities and counties receive an annual production fee and 40 percent of payments for new capacity. Social acceptance problems remain. The industry is described as subject to 344 paragraphs. Special schemes except development licences largely went under the radar until 2015.

### Uncertainty and limitations
This is a synthesis of the licence architecture, not a new dataset on how many special-purpose licences leak into commercial production. Effects after conversion of development licences are called highly uncertain. The traffic-light system is described as still lacking general legitimacy.

### Relevance to this corpus
Separates the right to produce from the right to a site, which is the legal frame for area access.
