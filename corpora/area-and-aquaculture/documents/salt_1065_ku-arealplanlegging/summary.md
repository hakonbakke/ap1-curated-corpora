### Background
The Ministry of Local Government and Regional Development (KDD, case 22/813) asked SALT to check whether newer coastal and sea-area plans actually settle uses, especially aquaculture versus other interests, and how knowledge is used in impact assessments. Municipalities and regional authorities still found this hard despite newer sea-planning guidance. Two Parliamentary Ombudsman statements (Brønnøy and Alver) had just asked how detailed municipal-level aquaculture assessment must be. The report is SALT 1065, dated 20 March 2023, from work between September 2022 and March 2023. An extra assignment during the project is folded into the same report.

### Methods
The authors call the work a qualitative governance analysis. They studied seven adopted plans through documents and interviews. The cases are inter-municipal plans for Helgeland (except Vega), Romsdalsfjorden and Nordmøre, municipal sea-area plans in Lebesby and Kvænangen, and zoning plans for Syltefjorden in Båtsfjord (sea) and Lundevågen in Farsund (land-based). Eight semi-structured interviews plus one group interview gave nine plan-side informants in five of the seven plans. Three county and two County Governor staff and one researcher were also interviewed. A digital meeting on 18 January 2023 had 16 sector and ministry participants. About 18 later aquaculture applications were read to see how some plans worked after adoption. Kvænangen and Lundevågen had no plan-side interview.

### Main findings
Planning clarifications between aquaculture and other interests can still be better. Some dispensation cases look like rematches or applications on sea not designated for aquaculture. Some operators appear to wait for dispensation rather than propose sites into the plan. Impact-assessment themes are often described well, but assessments of environmental effects are weaker. From the plan documents it is hard to see how themes were scoped, how interests were weighed, and how uncertainty was handled. Decision papers should state more clearly how the impact-assessment regulations, Nature Diversity Act sections 8-12, and the Water Regulations were applied. Table 6 marks water environment as not described or assessed in the municipal-plan column.

Most municipal and inter-municipal processes took a long time (around six years for Helgeland, Romsdalsfjorden and Nordmøre). Knowledge can go stale, and aquaculture applications appear while the plan is still open. Nature diversity is the knowledge gap named most often.

Helgeland used the reverse planning principle plus some single-use zones. Fourteen municipalities started. Three left. Vega is excluded. Rødøy still sits on a 2001 plan. A 20 m depth rule was the compromise that allowed multi-use aquaculture. After adoption, Varpet was licensed after an earlier dispensation was overturned, Risvær was refused despite being in the plan (eagle owl), and a Lyng cod application split Lurøy from Nordland county on whether a provision was binding.

Romsdalsfjorden used single-use zones and reduced total aquaculture area. A later closed-pen salmon site needed dispensation because the zone had been set for marine species. Nordmøre used multi-use only, barred new anadromous farms in two fjord areas, and limited two threshold fjords to aquaculture without feed or discharge. A site later proved ice-covered in winter because one municipality skipped a public meeting.

Lebesby moved to single-use zones, required zoning plans for six new areas, and asked for year-long current measurements. A Sámi objection was settled by KDD on 16 February 2021. Parallel zoning and Aquaculture Act treatment then met two applicants. The Act is not first-in-time. Kvænangen allows salmonids only. Spildra øst was taken out of the plan, then given dispensation, then overturned as undermining the new plan. An ILA 10 km zone in 2019 drove later dispensation attempts.

Syltefjorden is a private SalMar zoning case. An appeal over wild salmon and Arctic char was not upheld on 24 January 2022. The locality licence was still pending. Lundevågen is land-based. The zoning map and provisions are called incomplete. A 24 000 tonne a year permit was issued on 21 December 2021.

The Ombudsman statements say municipal-plan environmental assessment of aquaculture cannot be postponed to licensing, and that the traffic-light system is not a substitute. Multiconsult 2021 is cited for only 13 formal impact assessments among about 1200 aquaculture sites.

Recommendations cover four bundles: more resources and better organisation of planning (informants named Havbruksfondet or resource-rent tax as possible earmarks), what to assess at municipal-plan versus zoning versus Aquaculture Act licensing (single-use zones large enough for later technology, multi-use not used to postpone conflict), whether planning plus aquaculture administration together meet EU strategic and project assessment duties (including how production areas and traffic lights fit), and stronger environmental knowledge plus more standardised sea-assessment guidance.

### Uncertainty and limitations
Seven purposive cases, not a census. Some assessments are said to be unrealistic to expect from municipalities at plan level (total lice load, genetic change, total nutrients in a fjord). Sámi coastal fishing still lacks a working definition. Table 6 and table 7 are author judgements.
