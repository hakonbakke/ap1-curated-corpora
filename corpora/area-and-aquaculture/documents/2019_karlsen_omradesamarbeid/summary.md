### Background
Karlsen, Robertsen, Hersoug, Tveterås and Osmundsen study area collaboration in Norwegian aquaculture: cooperation between two or more firms in a defined sea area. The government vision of large growth by 2050 is used as context. Shared water carries feed waste, disease and lice, so one firm's choices affect neighbours. The question is whether such cooperation should stay voluntary or be placed with the authorities.

### Methods
An initial 2018 mapping used interviews or a survey with 37 informants from industry, authorities and organisations, plus regulation review. Two Food Safety Authority staff were interviewed in autumn 2018. In 2019, Norstat ran a national survey of 37 salmon and trout farmers (phone and web), approved by NSD. Mattilsynet internal data were used to digitise fallowing areas as of June 2019. A simple bioeconomic sketch (Misund et al. 2019) is presented, not estimated. The 2018 mapping and the 2019 farmer survey are two different N=37 samples.

### Main findings
Most mapped collaborations are voluntary. Several informants describe 'voluntary under compulsion': firms cooperate because they expect an order if they do not. Mandated themes include fallowing, zone cooperation, fish health, lice and disease, joint operation and co-location, and regional planning strategy.

Survey respondents largely cooperate with neighbouring farmers on operations and preparedness. About two thirds also cooperate with authorities beyond mandatory requirements. Twenty-eight of 37 cooperate to reduce disease and parasite risk. Twenty-three of 37 report good cooperation with both large and small firms. Two report poor cooperation. Two are alone in their zone. Conflicts are reported more often where many firms share an area and sites are dense. Twenty-three of 37 agreed that competition for sites limits operational cooperation. The authors say it is hard to conclude whether large or small firms cooperate more.

Twenty-five informants said the Food Safety Authority requires coordinated fallowing when approving operating plans. Practice is said to vary between regions. Over half treated coordinated stocking and fallowing as important. About half did not treat coordinated delousing as central.

The 2019 toxic algae bloom in Nordland and Troms is used to show that site-level plans can fail when many farms need the same local suppliers at once. A companion report is cited for about 13 400 tonnes and about 8 million fish lost among 14 farmers. Recommendations include keeping collaboration voluntary, writing clearer contracts with visible actions and sanctions, better well-boat and dead-fish rules, a conflict-handling arrangement, a minimum of emergency sites in each production area, more knowledge on joint fallowing (Guarracino et al. 2018 raise doubt that it cuts lice), and digital tools for combined impacts and fallowing maps.

### Uncertainty and limitations
N=37 on the farmer survey. Some farmers were reluctant during the algae bloom. The fallowing map is a snapshot. Several survey figures in the extract are images, so some counts are incomplete. The bioeconomic model lacks quantitative epidemiological parameters. Respondents disagree on who should own the cooperation.

### Relevance to this corpus
This is a core text on how shared water is coordinated between firms, and why site competition can block that coordination.
