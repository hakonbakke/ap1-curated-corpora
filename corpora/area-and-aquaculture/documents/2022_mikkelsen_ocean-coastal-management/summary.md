### Background
Mikkelsen, Sørdahl and Solås study strategic environmental assessments (SEAs) used in Norwegian coastal zone planning to judge how new or changed aquaculture area would affect other uses and values. They treat transparency and consistency as conditions for throughput legitimacy. Municipalities plan nearshore sea within 1 nautical mile from the baselines. Municipal councils make the final plan decision. A later site licence still needs sector permissions for pollution, food safety and marine traffic. The authors cite that environmental impact assessments at that licence stage are rarely made.

Four intermunicipal processes ran in Troms in 2013-2016. Mid and South Troms is described as a nationally cited good example. The Tromsø region process was led by a consultant whose practice the authors say is common elsewhere.

### Methods
Two processes are compared: Mid and South Troms (13 municipalities, municipal planners, 22 scored themes) and the Tromsø region (five municipalities, one consultant, five scored themes plus verbal themes). Together they cover 18 municipalities and 143 proposed aquaculture areas. The authors classify listed data sources, code whether scoring rules are explicit, fit probit models of recommend or allow decisions, and compare verbal summaries with theme scores. Interviews inform context and are not used as coded data. Participation is not studied.

### Main findings
Mid and South Troms proposed 109 aquaculture areas (101 salmon: 41 new, 60 expansions). At the start of planning these municipalities produced about 70 000 tonnes of salmon, about half of county production, from 85 approved localities (61 salmon). The SEA recommended allowing 89 and disallowing 20. Politicians later turned down 8 recommendations and allowed 5 areas the SEA had not recommended.

The Tromsø region proposed 34 areas (33 salmon: 31 new, 2 expansions) against 22 approved salmon localities. The SEA recommended 15 allow and 19 disallow. Politicians later turned down 4 recommendations and allowed 4 areas the SEA had not recommended. Jobs or income were not an explicit scored theme there.

Both SEAs list similar external sources (46 listings in Mid and South Troms, 51 in the Tromsø region). National databases are listed most often, usually only by name. Public meetings appear twice as often in Mid and South Troms. Most value and impact scores rest on discretion. In Mid and South Troms only six of 22 themes had a clearly stated value-score rule, and only two themes had clear rules for both value and impact. Neither SEA states a general rule for recommend or not recommend.

Models suggest Tromsø-region recommendations tracked the sum of consequence scores more consistently. Mid and South Troms fit better with extreme theme scores. Political decisions went against 24 percent of Tromsø recommendations and 12 percent of Mid and South Troms recommendations.

Verbal summaries often understated negative scores. In Mid and South Troms, 108 of 110 summaries had discrepancies (mean 4.3, highest 13). Ninety percent of discrepancies made consequences look less negative. Stocks of anadromous salmonids were misstated in 65 percent of those summaries, spawning areas in 38 percent, and landscape in 35 percent. The Tromsø region had fewer discrepancies (19 of 34 summaries, mean 0.8), and all of them made consequences look less negative.

The authors argue that more complete set-ups can raise transparency and cross-process consistency, but added complexity and black-box sources can reduce experienced transparency. Rigid standardisation can miss local context. Verbal summaries should be quality-assured. Formal objections from sector agencies must still be resolved before a plan can pass.

### Uncertainty and limitations
Two cases in one county. The authors cannot tell whether inferred decision rules were used consciously, or whether summary bias changed political votes. Some models did not converge. Theme scores may not be comparable across themes. If only large scores drive decisions, small consequences that add up across many areas may be ignored. Supplementary tables are cited and not in the extract.
