### Background
Robertsen, Kvalvik, Andreassen, Hersoug and Johnsen (Nofima report 27/2014, October 2014) ask whether inter-municipal coastal-zone planning is a better tool for access to aquaculture sites. Small coastal municipalities often lack planning capacity. Sector rules on disease distance and the environment also make single-municipality plans a poor fit for fjords that cross borders.

### Methods
Document review, interviews with leaders of inter-municipal projects, a survey of aquaculture firms, and six cases: Kysten er klar, Troms, Kystplan Helgeland, Romsdalsfjorden, Sjøområder Nordmøre and Fensfjorden. The farmer survey went to 111 firms and received 54 replies.

### Main findings
The Directorate of Fisheries is cited for the point that most coastal municipalities have a coastal zone plan, while many plans are old and still show aquaculture areas that are no longer usable. Sixty-five of 157 municipalities that host salmon or trout localities were engaged in inter-municipal sea planning, in six projects.

Of firms that had joined a plan process, the firm's interest conflicted with conservation (57 percent), fisheries (54 percent), wild salmon (36 percent), sea transport (22 percent), tourism (19 percent) and other farmers (11 percent). Ninety-three percent of respondents had taken part in a plan in the previous six years. Time spent was on average more than one week and less than one month. Contact with politicians and the administration was mostly described as good. Fewer said that politicians took the input into account. Just over half found the administrative handling competent.

The processes are project-organised. They share data, routines and competence, and they look at larger sea areas. The municipal council still adopts the plan for its own sea. The authors say this keeps a piece-by-piece pattern. Continuity after the project ends is weak. They cannot give a single answer on site access. Fensfjorden designated no new aquaculture areas. Nord-Troms cleared ten new aquaculture areas. Kysten er klar used a reversed principle with multi-use areas that include aquaculture. Several farmers report refusals even for applications aimed at areas already zoned for aquaculture.

On the knowledge base, Nature Diversity Act sections 8 to 10 are named (knowledge, precaution, ecosystem approach and cumulative load). National databases cover nature, traffic and pollution. The projects received little corresponding fact base from the firms on local economic effects.

### Uncertainty and limitations
Several projects were still underway. The authors say it is early to conclude. The survey figures describe the responding firms. They are not a census of all site applications.

### Relevance to this corpus
The report is evidence on how site access, municipal planning and conflicts with other users interact, and on what knowledge those plans actually use. It does not estimate disease transmission between farms.
