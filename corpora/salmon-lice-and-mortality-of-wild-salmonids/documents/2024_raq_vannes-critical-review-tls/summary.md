### Background
This critical review by van Nes, Imsland, and Jones (2024) evaluates the Norwegian Traffic Light System (TLS) used to manage the impact of salmon lice from aquaculture on wild Atlantic salmon populations. The TLS is a regulatory framework that uses observational data and models to estimate the risk of lice-induced mortality in wild salmon, influencing aquaculture capacity regulations.

### Methods
The authors conducted a systematic review of scientific and grey literature relevant to the TLS, focusing on salmon lice biology, environmental factors, and smolt behavior. They assessed the reliability of observational data, the estimation of lice larvae production from farms, model predictions of lice dispersal and infection, and the interpretation of lice burden into mortality effects.

### Main Findings
1. The timing and duration assumptions of smolt migration in the TLS contribute to overestimates of infection pressure.
2. Lice larvae production from farmed salmon is overestimated.
3. TLS models rely on potentially flawed data for calibration.
4. Lice-associated mortality in wild salmon smolts may be overestimated.
5. Lice infection levels on farms are not associated with measurable effects on wild salmon.

### Uncertainty and Limitations
The review highlights significant uncertainties in the TLS, particularly regarding the timing and duration of smolt migration and the assumptions used in model calibration. The authors suggest that these uncertainties lead to unreliable estimates of lice-induced mortality.

### Relevance to this Corpus
This review is relevant to questions about the robustness of the TLS (Q10) and the translation of infestation levels to mortality estimates (Q7). It critiques the methodology used in the TLS and suggests improvements to enhance its accuracy and reliability.
