### Background
The study by Jansen et al. (2025) investigates the effects of salmon lice from aquaculture on the numbers and size distributions of Atlantic salmon returning to spawn in Norwegian rivers. This issue has been controversial, with concerns about the impact of lice from farms on wild salmonid populations.

### Methods
The researchers used a log-normal regression model to analyze data on returning mature salmon from 104 Norwegian rivers over the period 2019–2024. The model incorporated theoretical smolt production, river catches from a previous generation, and parasite-induced mortality (PIM) of out-migrating post-smolts.

### Main findings
The study found a significant negative effect of PIM on the returns of one-sea-winter (SW) salmon, a non-significant negative effect on two SW salmon, and a significant positive effect on three SW salmon, though from low numbers. Overall, PIM was associated with reduced returns of mature salmon, particularly affecting smaller size classes. The study suggests that salmon lice from farms may reduce the numbers and alter the size distribution of returning salmon.

### Uncertainty and limitations
The study acknowledges uncertainties due to assumptions about the relationship between salmon size and sea-age, potential misclassification of salmon, and the independence of random noise in the model. The filtering of data based on judgment may also introduce bias, although sensitivity analyses suggest robustness in the main findings.

### Relevance to this corpus
This study is relevant to questions about the impact of aquaculture-derived salmon lice on wild salmonid populations, particularly regarding the translation of infestation levels to mortality estimates (Q7) and the population-level impact of lice-induced mortality (Q9).

### Relation to other documents
The findings support previous studies indicating negative impacts of salmon lice on wild salmonids, such as those by Larsen et al. (2024) and Vollset et al. (2023). However, it contrasts with studies questioning the severity of these effects, like Jansen & Gjerde (2021).
