### Background
This study investigates the effects of salmon louse (Lepeophtheirus salmonis) infection on osmoregulation, growth, and survival in Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar) under laboratory conditions. The research aims to define threshold values for lice infection intensity that lead to physiological stress responses in Atlantic salmon post-smolts.

### Methods
The experiment involved infecting first-generation wild-origin Atlantic salmon post-smolts with salmon lice copepodids and comparing them to uninfected controls. The study measured specific growth rates (SGR), plasma ion levels (Na+, Cl−), osmolality, and cortisol over a 28-day period. Infection intensity thresholds for physiological responses were calculated using regression analysis.

### Main findings
- Infected post-smolts had a mean infection intensity of 0.38 lice g−1 fish weight.
- Infected fish showed higher plasma Na+, Cl−, and osmolality, indicating osmoregulatory impairment.
- Specific growth rates were lower in infected fish (0.4% day−1) compared to controls (1.0% day−1).
- Mortality was positively correlated with infection intensity, with moribund fish occurring at intensities above 0.2 lice g−1.
- Calculated threshold values for plasma ion changes were 0.18 lice g−1 for Cl− and 0.22 lice g−1 for Na+.

### Uncertainty and limitations
The study was conducted under controlled laboratory conditions, which may not fully replicate natural environments. The infection success rate was 69%, and the study did not explore temperature effects on host response.

### Relevance to this corpus
This research provides critical threshold values for physiological stress in Atlantic salmon due to lice infestation, contributing to understanding the physiological effects of infestation on wild fish (Q6) and translating infestation levels to mortality estimates (Q7).

### Relation to other documents
The study aligns with findings from Grimnes and Jakobsen (1996) and Finstad et al. (2000) regarding the physiological impacts of lice on salmon. It provides empirical data supporting the need for monitoring and conservation efforts in regions with high lice infestation levels.
